UNIVERSAL MUNICIPAL GRANT ELIGIBILITY FRAMEWORK
CivicGrant IQ Knowledge Base | FY2026 Edition
Applicable to: All U.S. Municipalities, Counties, Townships, and Special Districts

=============================================================================
PART 1: CITY CLASSIFICATION SYSTEM
=============================================================================

A municipality's size, geographic classification, and income profile are the
primary determinants of which federal and state grant programs they can access.
Use this framework to quickly classify any city and route to relevant programs.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
1A. POPULATION TIER CLASSIFICATION
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

TIER 1 — MICRO CITY / SMALL TOWN (Population < 10,000)
  Definition: Incorporated place with fewer than 10,000 residents
  Characteristics: Limited staff capacity, reliance on county/state services,
    high per-capita infrastructure cost, often rural or exurban location
  Primary funding sources: USDA Rural Development (dominant), state block grants,
    county CDBG pass-throughs, EDA, ARC/DRA regional programs
  Key eligibility advantages:
    - USDA Rural Development programs are exclusively available to cities/towns
      with populations under 10,000 (Water/Wastewater) or 20,000 (Community Facilities)
    - Lower competition pool vs. large city grant cycles
    - Many programs have "small community" scoring preferences
  Key challenges:
    - Insufficient staff to manage complex federal applications
    - Limited match capacity (no large reserve funds)
    - May not have SAM.gov registration or UEI number
    - State grants often require a minimum grant administration capacity

TIER 2 — SMALL CITY (Population 10,000–50,000)
  Definition: Incorporated municipality or urban area with 10,000–50,000 residents
  Characteristics: Professional management (often City/Village Manager), dedicated
    public works, moderate capital capacity, may or may not be in an MSA
  Primary funding sources: CDBG (if HUD entitlement or state-administered),
    USDOT RAISE, FEMA BRIC, EPA SRF, USDA CF (if rural designation), state DOT,
    state infrastructure programs, EDA
  Key eligibility advantages:
    - Below $50K population threshold for USDA Community Facilities "intermediate" rates
    - Often eligible for both rural AND urban program streams
    - Less competition than major metros
    - Strong benefit-cost ratios on transportation projects (lower baseline ADT = bigger gap)
  Key challenges:
    - CDBG entitlement threshold is 50,000 (HUD) — smaller cities rely on state-administered funds
    - May lack dedicated grant writing staff
    - Federal programs increasingly require Equity Plans and DEIA commitments
  Examples: Buffalo Grove IL (41,496), Bozeman MT (53,000), Naperville IL (148,000),
    Fishers IN (100,000), Round Rock TX (128,000)

TIER 3 — MID-SIZE CITY (Population 50,000–250,000)
  Definition: Substantial standalone city, often principal city of a micropolitan or
    smaller metropolitan area
  Characteristics: Full city government, dedicated planning/grants departments,
    HUD CDBG entitlement status likely, established capital improvement program,
    independent bond rating
  Primary funding sources: HUD CDBG (entitlement), RAISE, MEGA, INFRA, FTA,
    EPA CWSRF/DWSRF, FEMA BRIC/HMGP, EDA, DOE Grid, state DOT, state housing
  Key eligibility advantages:
    - HUD direct entitlement CDBG allocation (predictable annual funding)
    - Sufficient scale to pursue large RAISE/INFRA/MEGA awards ($5M–$25M range)
    - Dedicated staff for federal reporting and compliance
    - Bond rating supports match capacity proof
  Key challenges:
    - Competing with larger cities for discretionary awards
    - Federal reporting burden (DRGR, IDIS, etc.)

TIER 4 — LARGE CITY (Population 250,000–1,000,000)
  Definition: Major regional urban center, likely core city of a Metropolitan
    Statistical Area (MSA)
  Characteristics: Large bureaucracy, established grants office, multiple CDBG/HOME
    entitlements, active federal partnerships, complex multi-modal transportation
  Primary funding sources: HUD CDBG/HOME/HTF, FTA Capital, MEGA, INFRA, RAISE,
    EPA SRF, DOE Grid/BIL Energy, HHS, DOJ, FEMA, NSF Smart City
  Key eligibility advantages:
    - Highest federal allocations (formula-based)
    - Federal agency direct relationships (HUD, FTA field offices)
    - Earmarks and appropriations (congressional relationships)
  Examples: Denver CO, Memphis TN, Baltimore MD, Milwaukee WI, Albuquerque NM

TIER 5 — MAJOR METRO (Population 1,000,000+)
  Definition: Core city of a large MSA (e.g., Chicago, Los Angeles, New York, Houston)
  Primary funding sources: Direct federal appropriations, USDOT mega projects,
    FTA New Starts, DOE hub grants, DOD grants, HHS, NSF, NCI
  Note: MEGA and other "transformational" programs require metro-scale justification

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
1B. GEOGRAPHIC CLASSIFICATION
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

RURAL (ERS Rural-Urban Continuum Codes 4–9; Census non-MSA):
  Definition (USDA): Outside of an urbanized area with population ≥ 50,000 AND
    outside of an urban cluster with population ≥ 10,000
  USDA Rural Development eligibility: YES (all RD programs)
  CDBG (non-entitlement): State-administered CDBG (CDBG-Non-Entitlement Communities)
  Key programs: USDA RD Water/Wastewater, Community Facilities, Rural Business,
    Rural Utilities Service, Reconnect Broadband, ARC, DRA, EDA, REAP, LIHTC

SUBURBAN (Within MSA, non-core city):
  Definition: Municipality within a Metropolitan Statistical Area that is NOT the
    principal city
  Examples: Buffalo Grove IL (suburb of Chicago), Plano TX (suburb of Dallas),
    Naperville IL, Scottsdale AZ (suburb of Phoenix MSA)
  Note: Suburban municipalities access the same federal programs as urban cities
    of similar size but may not get metro planning organization (MPO) priority
  Key access point: Must work with MPO (CMAP, NCTCOG, DVRPC, etc.) for STP/CMAQ funds

URBAN/METRO CORE:
  Definition: The principal city or central city of an MSA
  Access to: HUD entitlement, FTA formula, comprehensive planning grants

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
1C. INCOME PROFILE CLASSIFICATION
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

ECONOMICALLY DISTRESSED (MHI < $56,000 or < 80% of national median):
  National median HHI (2023 ACS): ~$78,700
  Programs with distressed community preferences:
    - HUD CDBG (LMI benefit requirement: 70% of activities must benefit LMI households)
    - EDA (Economic Distress Designation opens EDA Planning, Public Works, Economic Adjustment)
    - USDA Community Facilities (interest rate discount for low-income communities)
    - EPA Environmental Justice programs (EJScreen percentile ≥ 80 for bonus points)
    - DOE Justice40 Initiative communities (40% of climate investment benefits go here)
    - Treasury CDFI, New Markets Tax Credits
    - HHS Community Services Block Grant (CSBG)
    - USDA ReConnect (broadband priority scoring)
  Designation process: EDA economic distress = unemployment ≥ 1.5× national rate OR
    per-capita income ≤ 80% national average OR special need designation

LOW-TO-MODERATE INCOME (MHI $56,000–$80,000):
  Programs accessible: CDBG if LMI census tracts qualify, USDA moderate rates,
    HUD HOME, LIHTC, state affordable housing programs

MIDDLE INCOME (MHI $80,000–$120,000):
  Programs accessible: Most infrastructure programs without income-based preferences,
    RAISE, BRIC, SRF, state DOT programs
  Note: Buffalo Grove MHI $103,847 falls here

HIGH INCOME (MHI > $120,000):
  Challenge: CDBG LMI benefit documentation more difficult; income-targeted programs
    may require LMI census tract mapping rather than citywide MHI
  Strategy: Focus on infrastructure merit-based programs (RAISE, BRIC, SRF, HSIP),
    technical programs (NSF Smart Cities, DOE), and regional competitive programs

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
1D. SPECIAL DESIGNATIONS (unlock additional programs)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

OPPORTUNITY ZONE (IRS/Treasury):
  Definition: Low-income census tract nominated by governor, certified by Treasury
  Programs: Opportunity Zone Investment Fund (private investment), not direct grants
  Benefit: Attracts private investment with capital gains tax deferral; can be cited
    in CDBG/EDA applications as evidence of economic distress

PROMISE ZONE (HUD):
  Definition: High-poverty communities designated for federal coordination
  Programs: Priority access across 12+ federal agencies, dedicated federal liaison
  Active Zones: 22 designated across urban, rural, and tribal areas
  Application: Closed — existing designees use the designation for priority scoring

JUSTICE40 COMMUNITY (CEQ/OMB):
  Definition: Disadvantaged community as defined by Climate & Economic Justice
    Screening Tool (CEJST); EJScreen percentile ≥ 80th on multiple metrics
  Programs: 40% of benefits from climate/clean energy investments (IIJA/IRA)
    must flow to Justice40 communities; scoring bonus in BRIC, PROTECT, EPA EJ, DOE
  How to check: https://screeningtool.geoplatform.gov/en/

COAL / ENERGY COMMUNITY (IRA/IIJA):
  Definition: Census tract or MSA with significant coal employment or recent
    coal/power plant closure; brownfield communities
  Programs: IRA bonus credits for clean energy investment (10% bonus ITC for siting
    clean energy in energy community); DOE fossil fuel transition grants,
    EDA Economic Adjustment grants, ARC Power Grant Program
  How to check: IRA/IIJA Energy Communities mapping tool (energycommunities.gov)

TRIBAL COMMUNITY / TRIBAL LAND:
  Definition: Federally recognized tribal government or tribal trust land
  Programs: HUD IHBG (Indian Housing Block Grant), BIA, IHS Infrastructure,
    USDA Tribal Set-Asides, IIJA Tribal Transportation Program, EPA Tribal grants,
    DOE Tribal Energy Program. Many IIJA programs have tribal set-aside amounts.

APPALACHIAN REGION (ARC):
  Definition: 420 counties in 13 states from New York to Mississippi along
    the Appalachian mountain range
  Programs: ARC Area Development, Leadership & Workforce Development, Access to Capital
  Website: arc.gov

DELTA REGION (DRA):
  Definition: 252 counties and parishes across 8 states along the Mississippi Delta
  Programs: DRA Access to Capital, Public Infrastructure, Workforce Development
  Website: dra.gov

COLONIA (USDA/HUD):
  Definition: Substandard housing community near the U.S.–Mexico border in TX, NM, AZ, CA
  Programs: CDBG-CV Colonia set-aside, USDA Colonia Water/Wastewater priority, HUD Colonia HOME

PART 2: UNIVERSAL GRANT READINESS CHECKLIST
=============================================================================

Every municipality pursuing federal grants must have these in place:

TIER A — MINIMUM REQUIREMENTS (must have before applying to ANY federal grant):
  ☐ SAM.gov Active Registration (System for Award Management)
      - All federal grant applicants must be registered in SAM.gov
      - SAM registration provides Unique Entity Identifier (UEI) — replaced DUNS in 2022
      - Registration expires annually; must be renewed 60 days before application
      - Allow 5–7 business days for new registration to activate
  ☐ UEI Number (Unique Entity Identifier) — issued at SAM.gov registration
  ☐ DUNS/CAGE Code (legacy, still used by some DoD programs)
  ☐ Authorized Organization Representative (AOR) in Grants.gov
  ☐ Grants.gov account (grants.gov — federal grants portal)
  ☐ State single audit compliance (if receiving ≥ $750K federal awards/year)
      - 2 CFR Part 200 (Uniform Guidance) compliance required
      - Single audit report must be submitted to Federal Audit Clearinghouse

TIER B — PROGRAM-SPECIFIC REQUIREMENTS (required for specific grant families):
  ☐ FEMA/DHS Programs (BRIC, HMGP, PDM, NFIP):
      - Active NFIP community participation (no lapsed policies)
      - FEMA-approved Local Hazard Mitigation Plan (LHMP) — must be current
      - CRS (Community Rating System) participation = bonus points
  ☐ USDOT Programs (RAISE, MEGA, INFRA, PROTECT, Safe Streets):
      - Benefit-Cost Analysis (BCA) — required for RAISE/INFRA; use BCA Toolkit v6
      - ADA Transition Plan (updated within 3 years)
      - Coordinated Human Services Transportation Plan (for FTA programs)
  ☐ EPA SRF Programs (CWSRF, DWSRF):
      - State drinking water/clean water project priority list placement
      - Environmental review (NEPA categorical exclusion or EA)
  ☐ HUD Programs (CDBG, HOME, HOPWA):
      - Consolidated Plan (5-year) and Annual Action Plan (CDBG entitlement)
      - Low-to-Moderate Income (LMI) area benefit data or income survey
      - Analysis of Impediments to Fair Housing Choice (AI) or AFH
  ☐ All Infrastructure Programs (IIJA/BIL):
      - Buy America/Build America Act (BABA) compliance plan
      - Davis-Bacon prevailing wage compliance
      - National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA) review
      - Federal labor standards compliance

TIER C — COMPETITIVE DIFFERENTIATORS (strengthen any application):
  ☐ Moody's / S&P Municipal Bond Rating (Aa2, AA- or better = top-tier credibility)
  ☐ Dedicated capital reserve fund with documented available balance
  ☐ Letters of support from state legislators, congressional delegation
  ☐ Intergovernmental agreements (partnerships with county, transit, state)
  ☐ Prior federal award compliance record (no audits, no findings)
  ☐ Equity/Environmental Justice community engagement plan
  ☐ Climate adaptation / resilience plan
  ☐ Complete Streets policy adopted by city council
  ☐ Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) Transition Plan (current)
  ☐ Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) published and approved
  ☐ Long-range transportation plan alignment (MPO TIP/STIP inclusion)

PART 3: GRANT READINESS SCORING BY CITY TYPE
=============================================================================

When evaluating any municipality's grant readiness, score these dimensions:

FINANCIAL CAPACITY (0–25 points):
  25: Investment-grade bond rating (Aaa/Aa1/Aa2 or AAA/AA+/AA), reserves > 15% of budget
  20: Bond rating Aa3/A1 or AA-/A+, reserves 10–15% of budget
  15: Bond rating A2/A3 or A/A-, reserves 5–10% of budget
  10: Non-rated or below investment grade, reserves 3–5% of budget
   5: No bond rating, reserves < 3% of budget, recent budget shortfalls

TECHNICAL CAPACITY (0–25 points):
  25: Dedicated grants manager + department heads with federal compliance experience
  20: Part-time grants coordinator, experienced public works/engineering dept
  15: No dedicated grants staff but city manager coordinates grant pursuit
  10: Volunteer or contractor-based grant writing
   5: No prior federal grant experience

PRIOR FEDERAL GRANT TRACK RECORD (0–25 points):
  25: Multiple prior federal awards, zero audit findings, active federal awards
  20: 1–2 prior federal awards, clean audit record
  15: State grant experience only, no federal history
  10: No grant history but capacity exists
   5: Prior award with compliance issues or findings

PROJECT READINESS (0–25 points):
  25: NEPA complete or CE, design ≥ 60%, right-of-way secured, local match committed
  20: Design 30–60%, environmental initiated, match identified but not committed
  15: Feasibility complete, design not started, match concept only
  10: Planning stage, no design
   5: Concept only, no design or environmental work

PART 4: HOW TO IDENTIFY GRANTS FOR ANY CITY
=============================================================================

STEP 1: DETERMINE CITY TYPE
  Use Part 1 to classify: population tier + geographic type + income profile + special designations

STEP 2: IDENTIFY PRIORITY FOCUS AREAS
  Common municipal priority areas and associated funding streams:
  
  TRANSPORTATION & ROADS:
    - USDOT RAISE (any city, $1M–$25M, competitive)
    - USDOT MEGA (transformational projects, $100M+)
    - USDOT INFRA (freight, highways, $25M+)
    - IDOT/State DOT (STP-Urban, HSIP, CMAQ, ITEP/TAP)
    - FHWA Safe Streets & Roads for All (SS4A)
    - FHWA PROTECT (climate resilience, transportation)
    - Carbon Reduction Program (CRP)
    - FTA (transit capital, bus rapid transit, rural transit)

  STORMWATER & WATER QUALITY:
    - EPA CWSRF (Clean Water State Revolving Fund via state agency)
    - EPA DWSRF (Drinking Water SRF via state)
    - EPA Lead Service Line Replacement (LSLR — IIJA funded)
    - EPA Emerging Contaminants / PFAS (IIJA funded)
    - FEMA BRIC (flood resilience, green infrastructure)
    - FEMA HMGP (post-disaster, structure elevation, buyouts)
    - Lake/county stormwater authorities (varies by state/region)
    - NOAA Coastal Resilience (coastal communities)

  AFFORDABLE HOUSING:
    - HUD HOME Investment Partnerships (entitlement or state)
    - HUD CDBG (housing rehab, code enforcement, demolition)
    - HUD Choice Neighborhoods (public housing transformation)
    - USDA RD Section 538 GRRHP (rural rental housing)
    - LIHTC (Low Income Housing Tax Credits via state HFA)
    - HUD Housing Trust Fund (HTF — lowest income)

  BROADBAND / TECHNOLOGY:
    - NTIA BEAD (Broadband Equity Access & Deployment — $42.45B via states)
    - USDA ReConnect (rural broadband, up to $25M/project)
    - NTIA Middle Mile (middle mile infrastructure)
    - E-Rate (schools and libraries)
    - FCC Emergency Connectivity Fund

  ENERGY / CLIMATE:
    - DOE Grid Resilience (IIJA — grid modernization)
    - IRA Clean Energy Investment Tax Credits (ITC/PTC — also for municipalities)
    - DOE Better Buildings Grants (energy efficiency in public buildings)
    - USDA REAP (Rural Energy for America Program)
    - EPA Greenhouse Gas Reduction Fund (GGRF — $20B via community lenders)
    - EPA Environmental Justice Collaborative Problem-Solving
    - HUD Green and Resilient Retrofit Program (GRRP)

  PUBLIC SAFETY:
    - DOJ Byrne JAG (justice assistance — law enforcement equipment, training)
    - FEMA BSIR (Building Safety Inspection Records / code enforcement)
    - DHS UASI (Urban Area Security Initiative — major metros only)
    - DHS BRIC (physical hardening, early warning systems)
    - COPS Office (community policing grants)
    - NHTSA Highway Safety Program Grants

  PARKS & RECREATION:
    - LWCF (Land & Water Conservation Fund via NPS)
    - USDA Forest Service Community Forest Program
    - NEA/NEH (arts & cultural infrastructure)
    - HUD CDBG (park improvements in LMI areas)

  ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT:
    - EDA Public Works & Economic Adjustment (distressed communities)
    - EDA STEM Talent Challenge, Regional Innovation Engines
    - SBA Economic Development programs
    - USDA Rural Business Development Grants (RBDG)
    - HUD Choice Neighborhoods, Brownfields

STEP 3: CHECK STATE-LEVEL PROGRAMS
  Every state has analogous programs administered through:
    - State DOT (transportation — STP, HSIP, local bridge programs)
    - State EPA/DEQ (water quality, stormwater, environmental cleanup)
    - State Housing Finance Agency (HFA) (affordable housing, LIHTC)
    - State Commerce/Economic Development (business attraction, brownfields)
    - State CDBG administrator (for non-entitlement communities)
    - Governor's Office of Broadband (BEAD subgrant administration)

STEP 4: CHECK REGIONAL PROGRAMS
  Metropolitan Planning Organizations (MPOs): STP-Urban, CMAQ, TAP
    - MPOs program federal transportation funds for their regions
    - Cities must get projects on the MPO's Transportation Improvement Program (TIP)
  Councils of Government (COGs): often administer CDBG pass-throughs
  Regional utility authorities: stormwater, water, wastewater grants
  County programs: counties often pass through state and federal funds

STEP 5: CONFIRM ELIGIBILITY AND APPLY
  1. Verify active SAM.gov registration
  2. Identify NOFO (Notice of Funding Opportunity) on Grants.gov or program website
  3. Review eligible applicants, match requirements, evaluation criteria
  4. Develop project narrative aligned with program priorities
  5. Submit via Grants.gov or program-specific portal (e.g., FEMA GO, USDOT RAISES)

PART 5: CITY-TYPE QUICK REFERENCE
=============================================================================

FOR ANY QUERY ABOUT A SMALL SUBURBAN CITY (like Buffalo Grove, Naperville, Fishers):
  Top 5 program families: RAISE/USDOT, FEMA BRIC/HMGP, EPA SRF, state DOT, CDBG (via county/state)
  Match advantage: Strong bond ratings and capital reserves are competitive differentiators
  Strategy: Lead with project readiness (design %, right-of-way secured) and prior award track record

FOR ANY QUERY ABOUT A SMALL RURAL CITY OR TOWN (< 20,000, outside MSA):
  Top 5 program families: USDA RD (all programs), CDBG-Non-Entitlement, EDA (if distressed),
    ARC/DRA (if in region), ReConnect (broadband)
  Match advantage: USDA programs have no competition from metro areas
  Strategy: Confirm USDA rural eligibility first, then apply to RD directly (no competitive NOFO required for some programs)

FOR ANY QUERY ABOUT A MID-SIZE TO LARGE CITY:
  Top 5 program families: HUD CDBG (entitlement), FTA Capital, RAISE/MEGA/INFRA,
    EPA SRF, DOE Grid/Energy
  Match advantage: Scale justifies large award requests; federal relationships exist
  Strategy: Align projects with federal administration priorities (Justice40, climate, equity, safety)

FOR ECONOMICALLY DISTRESSED COMMUNITIES (any size):
  Priority programs: EDA Public Works, HUD CDBG, USDA CF, DOE fossil fuel transition,
    EPA Environmental Justice, IRA Energy Communities bonus credits
  Strategy: Document distress designation (EDA, CEJST, Justice40) prominently in every application

FOR COMMUNITIES WITH FLOOD/STORMWATER CHALLENGES:
  Top programs: FEMA BRIC (green infrastructure, flood early warning, structure hardening),
    FEMA HMGP (post-declaration), EPA CWSRF (wet weather, green infrastructure),
    NOAA (coastal), Army Corps Section 408, SMC/county-level stormwater programs
  Eligibility checkers: Active NFIP + current FEMA-approved LHMP required for FEMA grants

FOR COMMUNITIES WITH TRANSPORTATION CHALLENGES:
  Top programs: USDOT RAISE, Safe Streets & Roads for All (SS4A), HSIP (safety only),
    CMAQ (air quality + congestion), STP-Urban (pavement, bridges), TAP (trails, bike/ped),
    PROTECT (climate resilience), Carbon Reduction Program
  Eligibility checkers: ADA Transition Plan, BCA for RAISE, MPO TIP inclusion for STP/CMAQ
