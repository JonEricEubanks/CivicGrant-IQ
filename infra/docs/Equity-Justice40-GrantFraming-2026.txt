EQUITY, JUSTICE40 & ENVIRONMENTAL JUSTICE GRANT FRAMING GUIDE
CivicGrant IQ Knowledge Base | FY2026 Edition
Applicable to: All Federal Grant Applications — Equity is now a top-weighted criterion

=============================================================================
SECTION 1: WHY EQUITY IS NOW THE #1 SCORING FACTOR
=============================================================================

Since Executive Order 13985 (Advancing Racial Equity, January 2021) and the Justice40
Initiative (EO 14008, January 2021), every major federal infrastructure program has
elevated equity to a primary evaluation criterion. In FY2024-FY2026:

  → RAISE: "Equity" was explicitly elevated to equal weight with Safety in FY2023+
  → FEMA BRIC: New 40-point equity category added in FY2024 (up from 0 in FY2022)
  → SS4A: Community engagement with disadvantaged populations is a required element
  → EPA SRF: Principal forgiveness mandated for disadvantaged communities (IIJA requirement)
  → EDA: Justice40-community projects receive priority scoring

CRITICAL INSIGHT: Even wealthy suburban cities can score EQUITY points if they:
  1. Have senior populations, residents with disabilities, or limited-English households
  2. Have LMI census tracts within or adjacent to the project area
  3. Serve transit-dependent households
  4. Document barriers to access created by existing infrastructure deficiencies
  5. Have engaged underserved populations in the project planning process

SECTION 2: JUSTICE40 — THE COMPLETE GUIDE
=============================================================================

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHAT IS JUSTICE40?
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Justice40 is a Biden Administration commitment (EO 14008) that 40% of the
overall benefits of federal climate and clean energy investments flow to
"disadvantaged communities." The Biden Administration defined this broadly
to include communities facing burdens from:
  → Climate change impacts (flooding, heat, wildfire)
  → Legacy pollution (industrial facilities, superfund sites)
  → Energy poverty (high energy costs as % of income)
  → Housing burden (high housing costs as % of income)
  → Transportation burden (high transport costs, limited transit)
  → Health disparities (higher asthma, cancer rates)
  → Economic distress (unemployment, low wages)
  → Education gaps (low educational attainment)

NOTE (2025-2026): The Trump Administration (January 2025) rescinded EO 14008
and formally ended the Justice40 Initiative by EO on January 20, 2025.
However, PROGRAM-LEVEL equity requirements built into IIJA statutes (passed by Congress)
remain in effect. The equity scoring in RAISE, BRIC, SS4A NOFOs is statutory —
it cannot be eliminated by executive order. Equity scoring criteria for FY2026 NOFOs
remain substantively in place under different nomenclature.
STRATEGY: Don't use "Justice40" language in FY2026-FY2027 applications.
  Instead use: "disadvantaged communities," "underserved populations,"
  "low-income households," "environmental justice communities," "equity lens."

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
CLIMATE AND ECONOMIC JUSTICE SCREENING TOOL (CEJST)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Website: screeningtool.geoplatform.gov
What it does: Identifies census tracts as "disadvantaged" based on 18+ burden indicators
  across 8 categories (climate, energy, health, housing, legacy pollution, transportation,
  water/wastewater, workforce development) combined with low income indicators.

HOW TO USE CEJST FOR GRANT APPLICATIONS:
  Step 1: Go to screeningtool.geoplatform.gov
  Step 2: Enter your project address or census tract number
  Step 3: If the tract is flagged as "disadvantaged" — you get full equity credit
  Step 4: If your project tract is NOT disadvantaged, check adjacent tracts:
    → Does your project CONNECT TO a disadvantaged tract? (bridge/road connecting areas)
    → Does your project SERVE residents from a disadvantaged tract?
  Step 5: Document your finding in the application:
    "Census Tract [FIPS code] is identified as a CEJST disadvantaged community
    based on [X burden indicator(s)]. The project directly reduces [specific burden]."

CEJST BURDEN INDICATORS MOST RELEVANT TO MUNICIPAL GRANTS:
  Transportation Burden (transit):
    → Indicator: % households with no vehicle + low-transit-access score
    → Relevant for: RAISE, SS4A, FTA programs
    → Document: "X% of households within 1 mile of project have no vehicle and limited transit"
  Flood Risk:
    → Indicator: Share of properties with ≥ 1% annual flood risk + low income
    → Relevant for: FEMA BRIC, HMGP, FMA, EPA CWSRF
    → Document: "X% of properties in the project watershed face ≥1% annual flood risk"
  Legacy Pollution (PM2.5):
    → Indicator: PM2.5 exposure + low income
    → Relevant for: CMAQ, EPA programs, SS4A
  Energy Burden:
    → Indicator: Energy cost as % of income ≥ threshold + low income
    → Relevant for: DOE EECBG, IRA clean energy programs

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
EPA EJSCREEN — ENVIRONMENTAL JUSTICE SCREENING TOOL
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Website: ejscreen.epa.gov
What it does: Calculates EJ Index = environmental burden × demographic factor
  for any Census Block Group in the US

HOW TO USE EJSCREEN FOR GRANT APPLICATIONS:
  Step 1: Enter your address or block group at ejscreen.epa.gov
  Step 2: Run an EJScreen report showing percentile rankings
  Step 3: If your project area has EJScreen percentile ≥ 80th → document as "EJ community"
  Step 4: Key indicators to cite:
    → Particulate Matter (PM2.5): relevant for transportation/air quality grants
    → Ozone: relevant for CMAQ
    → Superfund proximity: relevant for brownfield and economic development grants
    → Traffic Proximity/Volume: relevant for all transportation grants
    → Flood Risk: relevant for FEMA/EPA water grants
    → People of Color %: overall EJ designation factor
    → Low Income %: overall EJ designation factor

EJSCREEN LANGUAGE FOR APPLICATIONS:
  "The project area (Census Block Group FIPS: XXXXXXXXXXX) ranks at the [X]th percentile
  nationally for [indicator] according to EPA EJScreen (version 2.3). This designation
  confirms the project area is an environmental justice community as defined by 
  [program name] evaluation criteria."

SECTION 3: HOW TO DOCUMENT EQUITY FOR EVERY CITY TYPE
=============================================================================

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
APPROACH A: PROJECT-AREA LMI DOCUMENTATION (for CDBG and HUD programs)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
HUD provides pre-computed LMI data for every census tract in the US.
Website: hud.gov/program_offices/comm_planning/maps (LMISD dataset)

Step 1: Download HUD LMISD data for your county
Step 2: Identify census tracts within or adjacent to your project area
Step 3: If ≥51% of residents are LMI (income ≤ 80% AMI) → Area Benefit eligibility
Step 4: Calculate: "[X]% of the [Y] residents within the project area are low-to-moderate income,
  qualifying this project for Area Benefit designation under the National Objective [24 CFR 570.208(a)(1)]"

AMI (AREA MEDIAN INCOME) 2024 LIMITS (80% AMI thresholds for LMI designation):
  Chicago-Naperville MSA: 80% AMI = $74,950 (family of 4)
  Dallas-Fort Worth: 80% AMI = $70,400
  Houston: 80% AMI = $65,300
  Los Angeles: 80% AMI = $79,900
  New York: 80% AMI = $93,350
  National: Check HUD income limits: huduser.gov/portal/datasets/il.html

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
APPROACH B: SPECIFIC POPULATION DOCUMENTATION (for all programs)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Even if the overall city is middle-income, document specific vulnerable populations:

ELDERLY POPULATIONS (65+):
  → ACS Table B01001: Get exact count and % for project area
  → Relevance: ADA accessibility, pedestrian safety, transit dependency
  → Language: "The project's 0.5-mile service area includes [X] residents age 65+,
    representing [Y]% of the population. These residents face disproportionate risk from
    [LOS F intersection / flooding / utility outage] due to limited mobility and
    vehicle access." 
  → Programs that weight elderly: SS4A, RAISE (ADA compliance), FTA 5310, CDBG public services

RESIDENTS WITH DISABILITIES:
  → ACS Table B18101: Disability type breakdown
  → Relevance: ADA compliance, accessible pedestrian infrastructure, reliable utilities
  → Language: "[X]% of project-area residents (ACS 5-year estimate) have a documented
    disability. The existing infrastructure fails to meet ADA standards at [specific locations],
    creating documented mobility barriers for this population."
  → Programs: All transportation grants (ADA Transition Plan required); CDBG public facilities

LOW-INCOME HOUSEHOLDS (even in affluent cities):
  → ACS Table B19001: Household income distribution
  → Every city has LMI households — identify the census tracts where they concentrate
  → Language: "While the Village of [X]'s overall median income is $[Y], census tracts
    [list FIPS codes] within [0.5/1/2] miles of the project have [Z]% LMI households.
    These [N] households disproportionately rely on [pedestrian/transit/aging utility] infrastructure."

LIMITED ENGLISH PROFICIENCY (LEP):
  → ACS Table B16004: Language spoken at home
  → Relevant for: Community engagement documentation, CDBG programs
  → Language: "The project area includes [X] households where English is not the primary
    language, including [Y] Spanish-speaking, [Z] Mandarin-speaking households.
    The Village conducted bilingual public engagement sessions on [dates] in [languages]."

TRANSIT-DEPENDENT HOUSEHOLDS:
  → ACS Table B08201: Household vehicle availability
  → Language: "[X]% of households within 1 mile of the project have no vehicle,
    making them entirely dependent on pedestrian access, transit, and bicycle infrastructure."

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
APPROACH C: INFRASTRUCTURE DISPARITY DOCUMENTATION
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Document how EXISTING infrastructure creates inequities for vulnerable populations:

TRANSPORTATION DISPARITY:
  "The project intersection has no pedestrian signal, no ADA ramps, and no
  protected crossing. The nearest alternative crossing is [X] feet away.
  For a resident using a wheelchair or walker, this [X]-foot detour represents
  a [Y]-minute additional travel time each trip — a barrier documented in
  [number] ADA accommodation requests filed with the Village since [year]."

FLOODING/UTILITIES DISPARITY:
  "The [X] households most affected by the documented flooding in the project
  watershed have a median household income of $[Y], compared to the
  Village-wide median of $[Z]. Lower-income residents are disproportionately
  located in the flood-prone areas due to historical development patterns."

BROADBAND/TECHNOLOGY DISPARITY:
  "The [X] households in the project area lack broadband access at speeds
  needed for remote work or distance learning (< 25 Mbps download).
  Of these, [Y]% have household incomes below 80% of AMI."

SECTION 4: COMMUNITY ENGAGEMENT — DOCUMENTING YOUR EQUITY PROCESS
=============================================================================

Federal reviewers now require EVIDENCE of community engagement, not just claims.
What counts as credible engagement documentation:

STRONG EVIDENCE (full points):
  → Formal public meetings with sign-in sheets (document attendance + demographics)
  → Online survey with minimum 100 responses (include demographic breakdown)
  → Multilingual outreach: translated flyers, bilingual staff at meetings
  → Targeted outreach to specific populations: senior center, disability services, school
  → Community advisory committee with low-income/minority member representation
  → Partnership with Community Development Corporation (CDC) or neighborhood association
  → Partnership with local Head Start, food bank, or social services organization

MODERATE EVIDENCE (partial points):
  → Village website notice + single public hearing
  → Email blast to community list
  → Notice in local newspaper

WEAK EVIDENCE (few points):
  → "Public input was invited" with no documentation
  → Single meeting with no documentation of who attended
  → No translation/language access for LEP communities

HOW TO BUILD A STRONG ENGAGEMENT RECORD IN 90 DAYS:
  Week 1-2: Identify community organizations representing disadvantaged groups
  Week 3-4: Host community meeting at accessible location; provide childcare/food
  Week 5-6: Conduct online survey (Google Forms); promote through social media + schools
  Week 7-8: Follow-up meeting with specific group (senior center, disability advocacy org)
  Week 9-10: Draft equity impact summary; document all inputs
  Week 11-12: Include summary in application; attach sign-in sheets as exhibit

SECTION 5: DEIA IN GRANT APPLICATIONS (DIVERSITY, EQUITY, INCLUSION, ACCESSIBILITY)
=============================================================================

Many FY2026 NOFOs ask applicants to address DEIA in project implementation.
Key elements to include:

WORKFORCE EQUITY:
  "The project will be constructed using Davis-Bacon prevailing wage rates,
  ensuring living wages for construction workers. The Village will include a
  Section 3 economic opportunity clause in all contracts exceeding $200,000,
  requiring that 30% of new hire labor hours be filled by low-income residents
  from the project area." (Section 3 of HUD Act applies to HUD-funded projects
  but can be adopted voluntarily for all grant-funded construction)

SUPPLIER DIVERSITY:
  "The Village will conduct outreach to MBE (Minority Business Enterprise) and
  WBE (Women Business Enterprise) certified contractors for all prime and
  subcontract opportunities, consistent with [state] Executive Order [X] on
  supplier diversity."

ACCESSIBLE DESIGN:
  "All project elements will be designed to ADA Standards for Accessible Design
  (2010 ADA Standards) and Universal Design principles, ensuring full access
  for persons with disabilities including those using mobility devices,
  vision-impaired pedestrians (detectable warning surfaces), and
  hearing-impaired individuals (visual signal alerts at pedestrian crossings)."

vulnerable population]. The protected pedestrian crossing will enable [N] elderly and
disabled residents to safely access [destination] without vehicle dependency. ADA

SECTION 7: ADA COMPLIANCE AS AN EQUITY FOUNDATION
=============================================================================

Every federal infrastructure grant expects ADA compliance as a baseline.
But ADA also strengthens your equity narrative.

ADA TRANSITION PLAN:
  → Required: Every local government that employs 50+ people must have one
  → Must be updated every few years; some grants require it to be < 3 years old
  → Identifies ADA deficiencies in public rights-of-way (curb cuts, sidewalks, signals)
  → Reference it: "The Village's ADA Transition Plan (adopted [year]) identifies
    [intersection/facility] as a Priority-1 barrier removal. This project completes
    Priority-1 item #[X] by installing [specific ADA improvements]."

ADA SPECIFIC DOCUMENTATION FOR GRANT APPS:
  → Count accessible curb cuts installed vs. needed (data from ADA Transition Plan)
  → Document pedestrian detectable warning surface coverage
  → Map accessible routes connecting to transit stops, medical facilities, schools
  → Cite any ADA accommodation requests received (Freedom of Information) as demand evidence

ADA IMPROVEMENT COST ANALYSIS:
  "$[X] of the total project cost is dedicated to ADA improvements:
  [N] compliant curb ramps ($[unit cost] each × [count]),
  [N] accessible pedestrian signal units ($[unit cost] each × [count]),
  [linear feet] of accessible sidewalk reconstruction ($[per LF] × [LF])."
  This level of specificity signals to reviewers that ADA is a genuine project driver.
