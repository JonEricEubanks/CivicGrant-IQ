SECTION B: PROJECT NARRATIVE
=============================================================================

B-1: Problem Statement and Community Need

The Northwood neighborhood in Buffalo Grove has experienced recurring, severe
stormwater flooding for over two decades. Located in the northeastern portion of
the Village along the Lake County / Vernon Township border, the neighborhood sits
in a flat glacial plain with limited natural drainage capacity. The drainage
infrastructure serving the area was designed to 1970s standards and is inadequate
for the 2-, 10-, and 100-year storm events now documented by the National Weather
Service.

Between 2015 and 2023, the Village recorded 23 separate flooding incidents in the
Northwood neighborhood. The cumulative property damage from these events exceeded
$3.4 million in private losses and $890,000 in public infrastructure damage.
Residents have filed 47 formal drainage complaints with Public Works, and the
neighborhood was specifically cited in the Village's 2022 Storm Water Master Plan
as a Priority-1 remediation area.

The root cause is structural: the existing stormwater system was designed for
smaller storms and does not convey flows through three critical drainage pathways:
(1) Northwood Park wetland and detention basin, which has become choked with
sediment and invasive vegetation; (2) Ridgewood Pond, which lacks adequate outlet
capacity; and (3) the network of open roadside ditches on the unincorporated
Lake County side of the Northwood neighborhood boundary.

Critically, the drainage problem cannot be solved by addressing only one property
or one jurisdiction. This application describes a neighborhood-wide, multi-
jurisdictional solution that addresses all three pathways simultaneously through
coordinated construction between the Village of Buffalo Grove, Vernon Township,
and the Buffalo Grove Park District.

B-2: Project Description

Phase I of the Northwood Neighborhood Stormwater Improvements includes the
following major components:

Component 1 — Northwood Park Wetland Naturalization and Regrading ($2.1M):
The 4.8-acre detention basin at Northwood Park will be regraded to restore its
original design capacity. Invasive species (primarily Phragmites australis) will
be removed across 3.2 acres and replaced with native prairie plantings and
emergent wetland species. A new inlet structure will be installed to control
inflow velocity and reduce sediment loading. The naturalized basin will provide
an estimated 14 acre-feet of additional flood storage and improve water quality
through natural filtration of nitrogen and phosphorus before discharge to
Buffalo Creek (IL EPA 303(d) impaired waterway).

Component 2 — Ridgewood Pond Outlet Improvements ($1.4M):
The existing 18-inch culvert outlet at Ridgewood Pond will be replaced with a
36-inch reinforced concrete pipe with a gated control structure. The pond
embankment will be armored with riprap to prevent erosion during peak flows.
This improvement will allow Ridgewood Pond to accept flows from the Park District
detention basin during major storm events, providing an estimated 8 acre-feet of
additional temporary storage.

Component 3 — Ditch Regrading and Culvert Replacement ($1.8M):
Approximately 3,100 linear feet of open roadside ditches will be regraded to
restore design grades, remove accumulated sediment, and improve conveyance
capacity. Six undersized culverts (12-inch to 18-inch diameter, installed 1968-
1982) will be replaced with 24-inch to 36-inch HDPE culverts. All culvert
replacements will incorporate fish passage design per IDNR guidelines.

Component 4 — Pavement Reconstruction and ADA Upgrades ($5.8M):
Streets disturbed during underground utility construction will be reconstructed
to current standards. This includes: Northwood Drive (1,840 LF), Ridgewood Drive
(1,220 LF), Timber Drive (890 LF), and associated cross streets. All 14 existing
pedestrian curb ramps within the project limits will be brought into ADA compliance
per current PROWAG standards. Project includes new LED street lighting on
Northwood Drive.

Component 5 — Project Management, Engineering, and Contingency ($2.7M):
Includes preliminary engineering, final design, construction inspection, materials
testing, ROW acquisition (minimal — three easements from willing landowners),
construction staking, and a 15% construction contingency.

B-3: Alignment with SIIP Priorities

This project directly addresses all five SIIP Priority Areas:

1. Flood Risk Reduction: Phase I eliminates the 10-year flood risk for 234 homes
   currently in the 10-year floodplain. FEMA FIRM mapping will be updated after
   construction to reflect the reduced risk.

2. Water Quality Improvement: The wetland naturalization component treats an
   estimated 187 million gallons of annual stormwater runoff, removing an estimated
   2,840 pounds of nitrogen and 890 pounds of phosphorus per year from Buffalo
   Creek, a Lake County-designated priority waterway.

3. Multi-Jurisdictional Coordination: The project requires and achieves active
   coordination between four entities: Village of Buffalo Grove (lead applicant),
   Vernon Township (permit and right-of-way cooperation), Buffalo Grove Park
   District (access and maintenance agreement), and Lake County Highway Department
   (roadway permits).

4. Community Resilience: Post-construction, 234 properties will see a reduction
   in stormwater insurance premiums. The project supports Buffalo Grove's NFIP
   Community Rating System (CRS) classification, which currently saves Buffalo Grove
   residents $280,000 annually in flood insurance premiums.

5. Climate Adaptation: The naturalized wetland design incorporates climate-
   resilient planting palettes selected for Illinois's projected 2050 climate
   conditions (ISWS projections) and can accommodate 15% increases in storm
   intensity without additional infrastructure investment.

SECTION E: LONG-TERM MAINTENANCE PLAN
=============================================================================
Post-construction maintenance will be handled under a formal Operations and
Maintenance Agreement between the Village of Buffalo Grove and the Buffalo Grove
Park District (attached as Exhibit E). Key provisions:

- Annual inspections by licensed civil engineer
- Invasive species removal and native planting maintenance (Park District)
- Culvert and structure inspection (Village Public Works)
- Annual stormwater monitoring report to Lake County SMC for 5 years
- Funding source: Village Stormwater Management Fund ($85,000 annual allocation)

The Village has maintained all prior grant-funded infrastructure with zero
inspection deficiencies and zero grant compliance findings.
