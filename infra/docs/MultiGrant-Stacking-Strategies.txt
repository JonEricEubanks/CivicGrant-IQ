MULTI-GRANT STACKING STRATEGIES FOR MUNICIPAL PROJECTS
CivicGrant IQ Knowledge Base | FY2026 Edition
The Art of Fully Funding Projects by Combining Multiple Sources

=============================================================================
SECTION 1: WHAT IS GRANT STACKING AND WHY IT WINS HACKATHONS
=============================================================================

"Grant stacking" means funding a single project from multiple independent sources —
federal grants + state programs + local bonds + special financing + tax incentives.

WHY REVIEWERS LOVE GRANT STACKING:
  → RAISE: Applicants who demonstrate ≥ 20% local match from non-federal sources
    score higher in the "Local/Non-Federal Commitment" criterion
  → BRIC: "Leveraged Funding" is a 40-point category — stacked funding = more points
  → SS4A: Multiple funding partners signal community commitment
  → EDA: Leverage ratio (non-EDA dollars per EDA dollar) is a scored criterion

THE STACKING CONCEPT:
  Imagine a $20M project. You don't need one $20M grant — you need:
    $8M  RAISE grant (40%)
    $4M  CMAQ (20%)
    $2M  HSIP (10%)
    $3M  State DOT (15%)
    $3M  Local bonds (15%)
    ─────────────────
    $20M TOTAL PROJECT FUNDED ✓

THE ANCHOR GRANT CONCEPT:
  Every stacked project needs one large "anchor" grant that provides 40-60% of costs.
  Build the stack around the anchor:
    → Transportation projects: RAISE, INFRA, or MEGA is typically the anchor
    → Flood/resilience projects: BRIC or HMGP is typically the anchor
    → Economic development: EDA Public Works is typically the anchor
    → Water/wastewater: CWSRF loan is typically the anchor (not a grant, but very low cost)
    → Broadband: BEAD state subgrant is typically the anchor

SECTION 2: TRANSPORTATION PROJECT STACKING COMBINATIONS
=============================================================================

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
STACK A: MAJOR ARTERIAL / INTERSECTION RECONSTRUCTION
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Best for: Signalized intersections, major road reconstructions, bridge replacements

RAISE (anchor, 40-50% of project)
  → Federal share: up to 80% of RAISE-eligible elements
  → Eligible: Roadway reconstruction, bridge, intersection, ADA, multimodal

HSIP (Highway Safety Improvement Program, 90/10 match)
  → Apply separately through state DOT
  → Eligible cost: All safety improvements (signal hardware, signing, pavement marking)
  → Stack logic: RAISE covers the full project; HSIP covers the safety-specific costs
  → Most efficient match ratio in federal transportation (state DOT pays 10%)

CMAQ (Congestion Mitigation and Air Quality, 80/20 match)
  → MPO programs CMAQ through TIP/STIP process
  → Eligible: Signal coordination, bike/ped improvements, intersection capacity (in non-attainment areas)
  → Stack logic: CMAQ covers multimodal/ATC components; RAISE covers roadway

TAP/ITEP (Transportation Alternatives Program)
  → MPO/state competitive program, 80/20 match
  → Eligible: Bike lanes, pedestrian paths, safe routes to school, trail connections
  → Stack logic: TAP covers dedicated bike/ped infrastructure embedded in larger project

State DOT Local Road Improvement Program
  → All states have formula or competitive programs for locally-owned roads
  → Illinois: ITEP, STP-L, IDOT District programs; typical 50/50 match
  → Stack logic: State fills gap between federal grants and local match requirement

Local Bonds (TIF, GO bonds, or revenue bonds)
  → Provide the required local match while freeing future tax receipts for other priorities
  → TIF: Tax Increment Financing — use future property tax increment to repay bonds
    (best for projects in older districts needing redevelopment)
  → GO bonds: General obligation; requires referendum in many states

EXAMPLE STACK — SIGNALIZED INTERSECTION RECONSTRUCTION, $5M:
  RAISE                $2,000,000 (40%) ← anchor grant
  HSIP                 $1,800,000 (36%) ← 90% federal, 10% local = $200K local match
  CMAQ (signal coord)    $800,000 (16%) ← 80% federal
  Local bonds (match)    $400,000  (8%) ← total local match across all programs
  TOTAL               $5,000,000 ✓
  Net local cost: $400,000 on a $5M project = 8% local share (extraordinary leverage)

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
STACK B: COMPLETE STREET / PEDESTRIAN SAFETY CORRIDOR
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Best for: Road diet, protected bike lanes, sidewalk gaps, pedestrian plaza

SS4A Implementation (anchor, 40-60% of project)
  → Specifically designed for pedestrian/bike safety projects
  → Lower competition than RAISE; $1M–$25M award range
  → Eligible: Everything in a complete street redesign

CMAQ (80/20 match, through MPO)
  → Covers air quality benefits of reduced car trips from mode shift
  → Best if project is in a non-attainment or maintenance area

TAP/ITEP (80/20 match)
  → Specifically designed for pedestrian and bicycle infrastructure
  → Strong program for sidewalk gaps, trail connections

Safe Routes to School (SRTS)
  → Available if project is within 2 miles of a K-12 school
  → Through state DOT; typically 100% federal for infrastructure
  → Stack logic: Designate school-adjacent project elements as SRTS-eligible

ADA Supplemental Funding (state programs)
  → Some states have separate ADA compliance funding programs
  → Illinois: IDOT has accessible pedestrian infrastructure funding available

EXAMPLE STACK — COMPLETE STREET CORRIDOR, $8M:
  SS4A Implementation   $4,000,000 (50%) ← anchor grant
  CMAQ                  $1,600,000 (20%) ← mode shift air quality benefits
  TAP/ITEP              $1,200,000 (15%) ← bike lane and trail connection
  Safe Routes to School   $800,000 (10%) ← near-school segments
  Local bonds             $400,000  (5%) ← local match
  TOTAL                 $8,000,000 ✓

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
STACK C: BRIDGE REPLACEMENT OR MAJOR BRIDGE REHABILITATION
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Best for: Structurally deficient bridges on local roads

Bridge Formula Program (BFP, 80/20 match)
  → IIJA created $26.5B dedicated bridge funding; most flows to states; some to local
  → Contact state DOT for off-system bridge replacement program eligibility
  → If your bridge is "structurally deficient" (SD) or "functionally obsolete" (FO):
    → Illinois: IDOT Off-System Bridge Program (OSBP) — apply to IDOT District Office
    → Texas: TxDOT Off-System Bridge Program — through district bridge engineer
  → Match: 80/20; state often provides the 20% match through their programs

RAISE (anchor if > $20M project, or if bridge is multimodal)
  → RAISE is better for major bridge replacements with multimodal elements
  → Can fund approach roadwork, pedestrian/bike lanes on bridge, utility relocation

FHWA PROTECT Program (PROTECT, 80/20 match)
  → New IIJA program for resilience and protection of transportation infrastructure
  → Eligible: Bridge replacement for resilience (if bridge is at flood/erosion risk)
  → Stack: PROTECT covers resilience elements; Bridge Formula covers structural replacement

State Historic Preservation Office (SHPO) Grants
  → If bridge has historic significance (NRHP eligible) — check with state SHPO
  → Transportation Enhancement funds available for historic bridge preservation

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
STACK D: TRAIL / GREENWAY / MULTIUSE PATH
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Best for: Off-road trails, greenway connections, linear park infrastructure

RAISE (anchor for > $5M projects with multimodal access connections)

TAP/ITEP (anchor for < $5M projects; 80/20 match)
  → Most trail projects start with TAP as the anchor
  → Apply through state DOT or MPO (varies by state)

LWCF (Land and Water Conservation Fund, 50/50 match)
  → Through state LWCF office (e.g., IDNR in Illinois)
  → Eligible: Outdoor recreation facilities, trail acquisition, park development
  → Cannot be combined with other federal funds on SAME project scope
    but can fund adjacent/complementary scope (trail acquisition vs. trail construction)

CDBG (if trail connects LMI neighborhoods)
  → Through HUD entitlement cities or state CDBG programs
  → If trail eliminates access barrier for LMI area → Area Benefit eligibility
  → Can cover design, construction, and land acquisition

Recreational Trails Program (RTP, 80/20 match)
  → Through state DOT, allocated by FHWA
  → Eligible: Non-motorized and motorized recreational trails
  → Good for nature trails, greenways without roadway connections

SECTION 3: FLOOD/STORMWATER PROJECT STACKING
=============================================================================

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
STACK E: STORMWATER MANAGEMENT / FLOOD MITIGATION PROJECT
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Best for: Detention basins, green infrastructure, creek/channel improvements

FEMA BRIC (anchor, 75% federal / 25% local for most applicants)
  → Best anchor for any flood risk reduction project
  → Eligible: Stormwater infrastructure, buyouts, elevation, natural systems
  → If CEJST disadvantaged community: 90% federal / 10% local match available
  → NOTE: Must apply through state hazard mitigation office (not directly to FEMA)

FEMA FMA (Flood Mitigation Assistance, 75-100% federal)
  → Specifically for NFIP-insured properties at repetitive flood loss
  → 100% federal for Severe Repetitive Loss (SRL) properties
  → 90% federal for Repetitive Loss (RL) properties  
  → Stack: FMA covers individual property buyouts/elevation; BRIC covers infrastructure

EPA CWSRF (Clean Water SRF, very low interest loan + possible forgiveness)
  → Eligible: Stormwater infrastructure, green infrastructure, CSO correction
  → "Disadvantaged community" forgiveness: up to 100% forgiveness of principal
  → Not a grant — low-interest loan (often 0.5-2% interest, 30-year term)
  → But low-cost capital reduces local debt burden; "stacks" by reducing true cost

USDOT PROTECT (Promoting Resilient Operations, 80/20 match)
  → For transportation infrastructure at risk from flooding or climate
  → If stormwater project protects a road, bridge, or transit — PROTECT applies

State/Regional Programs:
  → Illinois IEPA SIIP (Stormwater Infrastructure Improvement Program): $5.5M example
  → Illinois DCEO Community Development Block Grant for infrastructure
  → County stormwater management programs (often provide technical assistance + funding)

EXAMPLE STACK — REGIONAL DETENTION BASIN, $12M:
  FEMA BRIC              $6,000,000 (50%) ← anchor; 75% fed, 25% local
  EPA CWSRF loan         $3,600,000 (30%) ← low-interest, possible partial forgiveness
  State SIIP             $1,500,000 (12%) ← state program
  Local match/bonds        $900,000  (8%) ← local share of BRIC
  TOTAL                 $12,000,000 ✓
  Actual local bonds: $900K for a $12M project

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
STACK F: WATER / WASTEWATER INFRASTRUCTURE
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

EPA DWSRF (Drinking Water SRF) — anchor for water system improvements
  + EPA DWSRF Lead Service Line Replacement (IIJA dedicated funding)
  + USDA Water & Wastewater grants (if rural eligible)
  + USDA Emergency Community Water Assistance (post-disaster)
  + EDA Public Works (if project supports economic development)
  + State clean water programs (most states have supplemental programs)

EPA CWSRF — anchor for wastewater/stormwater
  + BRIC (if flooding contributes to CSO/SSO events)
  + State revolving fund supplemental grants
  + USDA RD Water & Environmental grants (if ≤ 10,000 pop rural)

SECTION 4: ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT PROJECT STACKING
=============================================================================

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
STACK G: BROWNFIELD REDEVELOPMENT
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Best for: Contaminated site cleanup and redevelopment

EPA Brownfields Assessment Grant ($500K, no match)
  → First step: Fund Phase I/II environmental assessments
  → Apply through EPA Brownfields program

EPA Brownfields Cleanup Grant ($500K, 20% match)
  → Fund actual cleanup of documented contamination
  → After assessment grant is complete

CDBG (if LMI area benefit or slum/blight removal)
  → HUD entitlement CDBG covers brownfield acquisition, demo, site prep in LMI areas
  → "Elimination of Slums and Blight" national objective works for vacant brownfields

EDA Public Works & Economic Adjustment
  → Fund infrastructure improvements to make cleaned site development-ready
  → Required: Documented economic distress in area; job creation plan

New Markets Tax Credits (NMTC)
  → Not a grant — tax credit equity investment
  → 39% subsidy on capital invested through CDFIs
  → Best for projects in NMTC-eligible census tracts (check CDFI fund)
  → Combines with EPA cleanup grants and EDA for full site redevelopment

Historic Tax Credits (HTC, if historic building)
  → 20% federal HTC + state HTC (varies by state; 15-25% in many states)
  → Stacks with NMTC for abandoned industrial/commercial buildings

Opportunity Zone (OZ) Tax Incentive
  → If brownfield is in an OZ (check opportunity.zones.gov)
  → Deferred capital gains taxes for investors → reduces equity financing cost
  → Not a grant but reduces deal financing cost significantly

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
STACK H: DOWNTOWN REVITALIZATION
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
CDBG (Public Facilities, Street Improvements in LMI area) — anchor
  + EDA Public Works (if economic distress present)
  + RAISE (if a major road/streetscape project)
  + TAP/ITEP (if bike/ped elements)
  + NMTC (for private development portion)
  + State Main Street / downtown revitalization programs
  + Façade improvement grants (local or state programs)
  + TIF district (provides ongoing public infrastructure funding from tax increment)

SECTION 6: THE STACKING METHODOLOGY — HOW TO BUILD YOUR STACK
=============================================================================

STEP 1: IDENTIFY YOUR ANCHOR GRANT (must be ≥ 40% of project cost)
  → Match the project type to the best anchor program (see Section 2-4 above)
  → Confirm eligibility; confirm you can meet the match requirement
  → This is your primary application; write it first and write it best

STEP 2: IDENTIFY COMPLEMENTARY PROGRAMS (cover remaining 40-50%)
  → Look for programs with no/low competition (formula programs, rolling applications)
  → Layer in safety-specific programs (HSIP) for their favorable 90/10 match
  → Use state programs to fill gaps and demonstrate state commitment

STEP 3: IDENTIFY LOCAL MATCH SOURCES (covers last 5-15%)
  → Bonds: GO bonds, revenue bonds, TIF bonds
  → Existing appropriations: Capital budget already allocated to project
  → County contributions: Regional project? Ask county to share local match
  → Utility contributions: Water/stormwater utilities can fund utility components
  → Special districts: Park districts, school districts, transit agencies can contribute

STEP 4: CHECK FOR FEDERAL STACKING PROHIBITIONS
  → Two grants cannot fund the EXACT SAME cost (double-dipping is prohibited)
  → Solution: Segregate project elements into distinct work packages:
    Roadway reconstruction → RAISE
    Signal safety upgrades → HSIP
    Bike/ped elements → TAP
    Environmental/air quality benefits → CMAQ
  → Have your engineer prepare a detailed cost breakdown by element type
  → Key rule: Each dollar of project cost can only be claimed by ONE federal program

STEP 5: SEQUENCE YOUR APPLICATIONS STRATEGICALLY
  Timing matters: Some programs require other grants to be committed first:
  → RAISE application: Submit before HSIP/CMAQ commitments are received;
    list them as "anticipated" with conditional commitments
  → BRIC: Submit leveraged funding commitments with signed letters; firm up before FEMA submission
  → CWSRF: Apply any time; EPA/state does not require grant commitment first
  → EDA: Often requires local match to be firmly committed before application

STEP 6: WRITE EACH APPLICATION TO REFERENCE THE FULL STACK
  → In every application, list ALL funding sources (committed and anticipated):
    "This project will be funded with RAISE ($3.8M), HSIP ($3.4M),
    CMAQ ($1.3M), and local bonds ($380K). HSIP and CMAQ applications
    are anticipated for submission in Q4 2026; local bond ordinance
    adopted by [City] Council on [date]."
  → This STRENGTHENS each individual application — shows project viability
  → Reviewers know a project with multiple committed funding sources is more likely to proceed

SECTION 7: FINANCING TOOLS THAT STACK WITH GRANTS
=============================================================================

TAX INCREMENT FINANCING (TIF):
  → State-created tool; available in most states
  → How: Freeze property tax base in a defined district; new property tax increment
    (growth above the frozen base) goes into a TIF fund for public improvements
  → Best for: Downtown redevelopment, commercial corridors, brownfields
  → Stacks with: CDBG, EDA, NMTC
  → Note: Cannot create new TIF districts overnight — takes 6-12 months to establish

SPECIAL SERVICE AREAS (SSA) / SPECIAL ASSESSMENT DISTRICTS:
  → Property owners in a defined area vote to assess themselves for improvements
  → Provides a dedicated revenue stream for area improvements
  → Stacks with: CDBG (for LMI areas), TAP (for streetscape)

REVENUE BONDS:
  → Water/sewer utility revenue bonds: Backed by utility revenues, not property taxes
  → Useful for: Water/wastewater projects where utility rate increases cover debt service
  → Low interest if issued as qualified bonds (tax-exempt)

FEDERAL HOME LOAN BANK (FHLB) COMMUNITY INVESTMENT PROGRAM:
  → Low-interest loans for housing and community development
  → Access through: FHLB member bank in your community
  → Stacks with: CDBG, HOME, LIHTC for affordable housing projects

ARRA (American Recovery and Reinvestment Act) — Historical Note:
  ARRA's "direct pay" bonds (Build America Bonds, QECBs) expired; however,
  IIJA created new clean energy bond programs — check with bond counsel
  on Qualified Energy Conservation Bonds (QECBs) for clean energy projects.

IRA DIRECT PAY (Inflation Reduction Act § 6417):
  → Municipalities (previously ineligible for tax credits) can now receive
    direct cash payments (IRA "direct pay") for clean energy projects
  → Eligible: Solar PV installation, geothermal heat pumps, battery storage,
    EV charging infrastructure, clean hydrogen, fuel cells
  → Amount: 30% Investment Tax Credit (ITC) base; up to 50% with bonuses:
    + 10% domestic content bonus (US-made solar panels)
    + 10% energy community bonus (census tracts with fossil fuel industry closure)
  → Stacks with: USDA REAP, DOE EECBG, state clean energy grants
  → Important: Apply for direct pay on Form 3468 with your annual tax return;
    no competitive application — this is an entitlement

SECTION 8: GRANT STACKING CHECKLIST
=============================================================================

Before submitting your anchor grant application, confirm:

  □ Project cost breakdown by element type (enables clean federal/state allocation)
  □ Anchor grant drafted with full stack listed in "Other Funding Sources" section
  □ Complementary grant applications identified with realistic timelines
  □ Local match source confirmed (council resolution or budget line item)
  □ Legal review: No double-counting of federal funds on identical cost items
  □ Each funding source's eligible use confirmed against project elements
  □ Congressional office notified of full project and all pending applications
  □ State program office notified of full stack (they may help coordinate)
  □ Engineer has prepared element-specific cost estimate (not just lump sum)
  □ Federal UEI/SAM.gov registration current and active
  □ Environmental clearances understood for each funding source (NEPA/state)
