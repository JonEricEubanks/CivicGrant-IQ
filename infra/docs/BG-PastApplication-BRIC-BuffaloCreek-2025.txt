SECTION 1: PROJECT ABSTRACT
=============================================================================
The Village of Buffalo Grove requests $3,400,000 in FEMA BRIC funding to
construct a flood warning and early notification system along the Buffalo Creek
corridor, install three green infrastructure rain garden networks at strategic
upstream collection points, and harden the Weiland Road lift station (critical
infrastructure) against 500-year flood events. Buffalo Creek runs through
3.4 miles of Buffalo Grove and discharges to the Des Plaines River; the creek
watershed covers approximately 12,200 acres and has caused property damage
exceeding $14.2 million in Buffalo Grove over the past decade.

SECTION 3: PROBLEM STATEMENT
=============================================================================

Buffalo Creek is a tributary of the Des Plaines River that runs through the
heart of Buffalo Grove for 3.4 miles. The creek is listed on the Illinois EPA
Section 303(d) list of impaired waterways due to nutrient loading and sedimentation.
The creek's 100-year floodplain encompasses 1,240 acres (12% of Buffalo Grove's
total land area) and includes 342 residential structures, 18 commercial buildings,
and two critical infrastructure facilities: the Weiland Road Water Reclamation
Plant and the Weiland Road Sanitary Lift Station.

Documented loss history:
  - 2023 July storm event: 5.8 inches in 24 hours; $2.1M property damage
  - 2022 October event: 4.9 inches in 18 hours; $1.8M property damage
  - 2020 August event: 7.2 inches in 36 hours (highest recorded); $4.6M damage;
    Weiland Lift Station flooded, causing 6-hour sewer system backup affecting
    1,240 households

Climate projections (ISWS, 2022): 100-year storm events are projected to increase
in intensity by 18-24% by 2050 under moderate emissions scenarios, compounding
existing drainage deficiencies.

The three specific vulnerabilities this project addresses:

VULNERABILITY 1 — Early Warning Gap:
The existing flood warning network (installed with 2020 HMGP funds) covers only
four monitoring points. Buffalo Creek has 11 tributaries with no monitoring.
The 2023 storm event caused significant flash flooding with only 20 minutes
warning time — insufficient for residents to protect property or evacuate.

VULNERABILITY 2 — Upstream Runoff Volume:
Three commercial/industrial sites in the upper Buffalo Creek watershed total
42 acres of impervious surface with no post-construction stormwater controls
(developed prior to modern Illinois stormwater ordinance requirements). These
sites generate an estimated 18% of the peak runoff that causes downstream
flooding during 2-year and larger storm events.

VULNERABILITY 3 — Weiland Lift Station Flood Vulnerability:
The Weiland Road Sanitary Lift Station, serving 9,400 customers (62% of the
village), sits at Elevation 634.2 feet NAVD88 — only 1.8 feet above the
100-year Base Flood Elevation (BFE). A repeat of the 2020 event would flood
the facility, interrupting sanitary service and triggering an IEPA Sanitary
Sewer Overflow (SSO) event affecting downstream waterways.

SECTION 4: PROJECT SCOPE
=============================================================================

COMPONENT A — Flood Early Warning System Expansion ($890,000):
Installation of 7 new stream gauges and weather stations at unmonitored tributary
confluence points throughout the Buffalo Creek watershed. Each station includes:
  - USGS-standard stilling well stream gauge (0.01-foot resolution)
  - Tipping bucket rain gauge with real-time telemetry
  - Solar power with 72-hour battery backup
  - Integration with existing ALERT2 flood warning network
  - Automated alert protocols to 911 dispatch, Village Manager, and residents
    registered for emergency notifications

Expected outcome: Warning time increased from 20 minutes to 90+ minutes for
affected areas. Early warning system serves the full watershed including the
Northwood neighborhood, Vernon Township, and portions of Wheeling, IL.

COMPONENT B — Green Infrastructure Rain Garden Networks ($1,840,000):
Construction of three large-scale rain garden networks at three commercial
sites (negotiated access agreements in place):
  - Site 1: Regency Square commercial center (11.2 impervious acres, north branch)
    Rain garden size: 0.8 acres; storage volume: 2.4 acre-feet; infiltration rate: 0.8 in/hr
  - Site 2: Deerfield Parkway industrial park (18.6 impervious acres, east branch)
    Rain garden size: 1.2 acres; storage volume: 3.8 acre-feet
  - Site 3: Milwaukee Avenue mixed-use district (12.4 impervious acres, main stem)
    Rain garden size: 0.9 acres; storage volume: 2.7 acre-feet

Native planting palettes selected from the Chicago Metropolitan Agency for
Planning (CMAP) Green Infrastructure Vision toolkit for climate resilience.
Total runoff reduction: estimated 8.9 acre-feet per 2-year storm event, reducing
peak flows at the Weiland Station confluence by approximately 24%.

COMPONENT C — Weiland Road Lift Station Hardening ($520,000):
  - Flood barrier (deployable aluminum stop-log system): Install around station perimeter
    to provide protection to Elevation 638.0 NAVD88 (5.6 feet above 100-year BFE)
  - Emergency generator upgrade: Replace existing 100-kW generator with 250-kW unit
    capable of operating all pumps at full capacity for 96 hours on full fuel tank
  - Automatic generator transfer switch with remote monitoring
  - Waterproof electrical conduit and junction boxes (NEMA 4X)
  - 48-inch elevated concrete equipment pad for electrical panels
  - Remote monitoring integration with Village SCADA system

SECTION 5: BENEFIT-COST ANALYSIS
=============================================================================
FEMA Benefit-Cost Analysis Tool (BCA-Tool 6.0) was used to calculate benefits.

Annual benefits:
  - Avoided property damage (residential, 342 structures): $1,240,000
  - Avoided commercial loss (18 buildings): $380,000
  - Avoided emergency response costs: $95,000
  - Avoided infrastructure repair (Weiland Lift Station): $280,000
  - Avoided SSO environmental penalty savings: $120,000
  Total annualized benefit: $2,115,000

Project life: 50 years
Discount rate: 7% (FEMA standard)
Net Present Value (NPV): $28.4M
Benefit-Cost Ratio (BCR): 6.68 — significantly exceeds FEMA minimum of 1.0

The BCR is calculated conservatively using current (2024) property values and
does not account for projected climate-related damage increases.
