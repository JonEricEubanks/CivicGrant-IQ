SECTION 1: U.S. DEPARTMENT OF TRANSPORTATION (USDOT) PROGRAMS
=============================================================================

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
RAISE DISCRETIONARY GRANTS (Rebuilding American Infrastructure with Sustainability and Equity)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
CFDA / Assistance Listing: 20.933
Agency: USDOT / Office of the Secretary
Authorization: IIJA Section 21201; annual appropriation
FY2025 Program Funding: $1.5 billion
Typical Award Range: $1M – $25M for rural; up to $25M for urban; no statutory max
Match Requirement: None required; competitive applicants typically show 20%+ local/state match
Application Cycle: Annual; NOFO typically published December–February; deadline March–May
Eligible Applicants: States, counties, municipalities, transit agencies, port authorities, MPOs, tribal governments
Eligible Activities: Road reconstruction, bridge replacement, multimodal connectors, port improvements,
  pedestrian/bicycle facilities, transit access, railroad grade separation, smart infrastructure
Evaluation Criteria (FY2025 priorities): Safety, environmental sustainability, quality of life, equity,
  economic strength, state of good repair, innovation, partnerships
Key Requirements:
  - Benefit-Cost Analysis (BCA) required; use USDOT BCA Toolkit v6
  - ADA Transition Plan (current)
  - Environmental review (NEPA)
  - Demonstrated local support (resolutions, letters)
  - Geographic distribution: ≥50% must go to rural areas; ≥50% to urban areas
Small City Notes: Cities under 200,000 population apply in the "rural" category. Rural awards
  are less competitive than urban — 150–200 rural applicants vs. 400+ urban.
Website: transportation.gov/RAISEgrants
Key Contact: RAISE@dot.gov

MEGA — NATIONAL INFRASTRUCTURE PROJECT ASSISTANCE
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
CFDA: 20.940
Agency: USDOT FHWA / USDOT
FY2025 Funding: $1.0 billion
Typical Award: $50M – $500M
Match: 10% minimum federal share cap (applicant covers 90% — very limited)
Note: For transformational, large-scale projects only. Not appropriate for cities < 500,000.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
INFRA GRANTS — NATIONALLY SIGNIFICANT MULTIMODAL FREIGHT & HIGHWAY PROJECTS
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
CFDA: 20.932
Agency: USDOT FHWA
FY2025 Funding: $1.55 billion
Typical Award: Large, highway and freight focus; minimum $25M; typically $100M+
Best for: Large highway freight corridors, interstate ramps, port road access

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SAFE STREETS & ROADS FOR ALL (SS4A)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
CFDA: 20.939
Agency: USDOT FHWA
Authorization: IIJA Section 24102
FY2025 Funding: $1.0 billion
Award Range: $100K–$25M; two tracks: Action Plan grants ($100K–$5M) and Implementation ($1M–$25M)
Match: 80% federal / 20% local match for implementation; planning grants may be 100% federal
Application Cycle: Annual; NOFO typically June–August
Eligible: States, local governments, MPOs, tribal governments
Eligible Activities: Vision Zero plans, road safety action plans, safety improvements (signals,
  crosswalks, speed calming, bike lanes), design changes proven to reduce fatalities
Key Note: This is the primary program for Vision Zero and pedestrian/cyclist safety projects.
  Implementation grants have lower competition than RAISE for safety-specific projects.
Website: transportation.gov/SS4A

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
HIGHWAY SAFETY IMPROVEMENT PROGRAM (HSIP)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
CFDA: 20.205 (sub-allocation by state DOT)
Agency: FHWA / State DOT
Authorization: 23 USC 148; IIJA Section 11108
Annual Funding: ~$3.5 billion nationally (formula to states, sub-allocated to locals)
Award Range: Varies by state; typically $100K–$5M per project
Match: 90% federal / 10% local
Application: Through state DOT; most states have annual call for projects
Eligible: Roadway segments with documented crash history (typically ≥ 3 crashes/5 years)
Eligible Activities: Signal upgrades, intersection reconstruction, median barriers,
  cable rail, rumble strips, curve delineation, pedestrian safety
Key Note: Most competitive for crash-heavy locations with documented injury/fatal crashes.
  Data required: 5-year crash history, ADT, LOS.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
CONGESTION MITIGATION & AIR QUALITY (CMAQ)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
CFDA: 20.205 (sub-allocation by MPO/state)
Agency: FHWA / MPO
Authorization: 23 USC 149; IIJA Section 11113
Annual Funding: ~$2.5 billion nationally (formula by nonattainment/maintenance area)
Award Range: $500K–$10M typical; accessed through MPO call for projects
Match: 80% federal / 20% local (some states vary)
Application: Through MPO (Metropolitan Planning Organization) Call for Projects, typically annual
Eligible: Cities in ozone or PM2.5 nonattainment or maintenance areas
Eligible Activities: Signal timing coordination, bicycle/pedestrian infrastructure,
  transit improvements, idle reduction, alternative fuel vehicles, freight efficiency
Key Note: Only available in air quality nonattainment/maintenance areas.
  Chicago MSA (including Buffalo Grove) qualifies (PM2.5 nonattainment).

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
TRANSPORTATION ALTERNATIVES PROGRAM (TAP) / ITEP
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
CFDA: 20.205 (sub-allocation)
Agency: FHWA / State DOT / MPO
Authorization: 23 USC 133(h); IIJA Section 11109
Annual Funding: ~$1.8 billion nationally
Award Range: $200K–$3M typical; highly variable by state
Match: 80% federal / 20% local
Application: State DOT or MPO call for projects; typically 2–3 year cycles
Eligible Activities: Bike lanes, shared-use paths, sidewalks, trail connections,
  safe routes to school, historic transportation preservation, ADA transition
Key Note: Illinois program called ITEP (Illinois Transportation Enhancement Program).
  Administered jointly by IDOT and MPOs.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
PROTECT GRANT PROGRAM (Promoting Resilient Operations for Transformative, Efficient, and
Cost-saving Transportation)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
CFDA: 20.936
Agency: FHWA
Authorization: IIJA Section 11105
FY2025 Funding: $8.7 billion over 5 years; ~$1.4B/year
Award Range: Formula (to states, then localities) + discretionary ($100M+ large projects)
Match: 80% federal / 20% local
Application: Formula funds allocated by state DOT; competitive grants through FHWA NOFO
Eligible Activities: Culverts, stormwater drainage, bridge scour protection,
  wildfire/flooding/earthquake resilience improvements to transportation infrastructure
Key Note: Excellent for road/bridge climate resilience. Can fund drainage improvements
  that also support stormwater management.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
CARBON REDUCTION PROGRAM (CRP)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
CFDA: 20.205
Agency: FHWA / State DOT
Authorization: IIJA Section 11403
Annual Funding: $1.6 billion/year (formula to states)
Award Range: Sub-allocated by state; typically $250K–$5M
Match: 80% federal / 20% local
Eligible Activities: Electric vehicle charging infrastructure on public roads,
  pedestrian/bicycle facilities, transit infrastructure, traffic signal coordination
Key Note: High priority in Illinois; IDOT running annual call for CRP projects.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FTA FORMULA PROGRAMS (5307, 5311, 5310)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Section 5307 (Urbanized Area Formula):
  Eligibility: UZAs ≥ 50,000 population; transit agencies
  Annual Funding: ~$5B nationally; formula by ridership + population
  Eligible: Operating assistance, preventive maintenance, capital projects
  Match: 80% federal / 20% local (operating: 50/50)

Section 5311 (Rural Area Formula):
  Eligibility: Areas < 50,000 population
  Annual Funding: ~$750M nationally
  Eligible: Rural transit operating/capital

Section 5310 (Enhanced Mobility for Seniors & Individuals with Disabilities):
  Annual Funding: ~$310M
  Eligible: Non-profits, transit agencies
  Eligible Activities: ADA-accessible vehicles, mobility management

SECTION 2: FEDERAL EMERGENCY MANAGEMENT AGENCY (FEMA) / DHS PROGRAMS
=============================================================================

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
BRIC — BUILDING RESILIENT INFRASTRUCTURE & COMMUNITIES
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
CFDA: 97.047
Agency: FEMA / DHS
Authorization: Robert T. Stafford Act §203; Bipartisan Budget Act 2018
FY2025 Program Funding: $1.0 billion
Typical Award Range: $100K – $50M; average $3–8M per project
Match Requirement: 25% non-federal cost share
Application Cycle: Annual; NOFO August–October; subapplication via state HSEMD; deadline January
Eligible Applicants: States (primary applicant); local governments through state
  (sub-applicants must go through state emergency management office)
Eligible Activities:
  - Flood control and resilience (green infrastructure, levees, detention basins)
  - Wildfire risk reduction
  - Utility hardening (generators, lift stations, water/wastewater)
  - Early warning systems (flood gauges, sirens)
  - Hazardous materials risk reduction
  - Building codes adoption and enforcement
  - Stormwater management improvements
  - Coastal resilience
Key Requirements:
  - Community must be active NFIP participant (no lapsed policies)
  - Current FEMA-approved Local Hazard Mitigation Plan (LHMP)
  - Benefit-Cost Analysis (BCA) ratio ≥ 1.0 (use Hazus-MH or BCA Toolkit)
  - Scope must be in current LHMP
Priority Scoring: CRS Class participation (+), equity/Justice40 designation (+),
  post-disaster community (-warning: HMGP is separate for post-disaster)
Small City Notes: Excellent program for any city with NFIP participation and LHMP.
  Suburban cities often win because they have strong match capacity and bond ratings.
Website: fema.gov/bric

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
HMGP — HAZARD MITIGATION GRANT PROGRAM
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
CFDA: 97.039
Agency: FEMA / DHS
Trigger: Post-disaster; activated after Presidential major disaster declaration
Available Funding: 15% of total FEMA disaster assistance per declaration
Award Range: Varies by project; typically $250K – $25M
Match: 25% non-federal cost share
Eligible Activities: Same as BRIC; additionally property acquisition/buyout,
  structure elevation (flood), wind retrofit, safe rooms
Key Note: Only available to communities in declared disaster areas.
  Active declarations: usafacts.org/articles/fema-disaster-declarations/

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FEMA NOFO — PRE-DISASTER MITIGATION (PDM) / FLOOD MITIGATION ASSISTANCE (FMA)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FMA — FLOOD MITIGATION ASSISTANCE:
  CFDA: 97.029
  Agency: FEMA
  FY2025 Funding: $800M (IIJA boosted this significantly)
  Award Range: No statutory cap; average $1–15M
  Match: 25% non-federal; 12.5% for severe repetitive loss properties
  Eligible: NFIP-participating communities; projects to reduce flood risk to NFIP-insured structures
  Eligible Activities: Structure acquisition, structure elevation, dry floodproofing,
    drainage improvements, green infrastructure to reduce flood insurance claims
  Key Note: FMA prioritizes repetitive flood-loss properties first.

SECTION 3: U.S. ENVIRONMENTAL PROTECTION AGENCY (EPA) PROGRAMS
=============================================================================

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
CLEAN WATER STATE REVOLVING FUND (CWSRF)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
CFDA: 66.458
Agency: EPA / State Environmental Agency
Authorization: Clean Water Act Section 603
Annual Funding: ~$1.6B/year federal capitalization (states leverage 5:1–20:1 with bonds)
Loan/Grant Type: Low-interest loans (1–2.5%); principal forgiveness available for small/disadvantaged communities
Repayment Period: Up to 30 years (20 years standard)
Eligible Applicants: Municipalities, utilities, special districts
Eligible Activities:
  - Publicly owned treatment works (POTW) upgrades
  - Stormwater management (CSO/SSO elimination, green infrastructure)
  - Non-point source pollution control
  - Watershed protection projects
  - Estuary restoration
Application: Through state environmental agency (e.g., IEPA in Illinois, TCEQ in Texas)
Key Note: Not a competitive grant — applied for through state revolving fund; projects
  placed on state priority list. Advantage: financing (not requiring full payback like bonds).
  Principal forgiveness (= grant) available for disadvantaged communities.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
DRINKING WATER STATE REVOLVING FUND (DWSRF)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
CFDA: 66.468
Agency: EPA / State Agency
Annual Funding: ~$1.1B/year federal capitalization
Similar structure to CWSRF; focused on drinking water infrastructure
Eligible: Public water systems
Eligible Activities: Water main replacement, treatment upgrades, storage, distribution
IIJA Lead Service Line Funding: $15 billion over 5 years for lead service line replacement
  — all public water systems with lead service lines are eligible; priority forgiveness

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
EPA ENVIRONMENTAL JUSTICE (EJ) GRANTS
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Environmental Justice Collaborative Problem-Solving (EJCPS):
  CFDA: 66.306; Award: $200K–$1M; competitive; for EJ communities
Environmental Justice Government-to-Government (EJG2G):
  CFDA: 66.312; Award: $100K–$600K; for tribal/state/local governments in EJ areas
Thriving Communities Technical Assistance Centers (TCTACs):
  Regional assistance for disadvantaged communities applying for environmental grants

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
BROWNFIELDS GRANTS (EPA)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
CFDA: 66.818 (Assessment), 66.819 (Cleanup)
Agency: EPA
FY2025 Funding: $200M (IIJA significantly boosted)
Award Range: Assessment: up to $500K; Cleanup: up to $500K per site
Match: 20% required for cleanup grants
Eligible: Municipalities, non-profits, tribes for brownfield site assessment and cleanup
Key Note: Excellent for communities with abandoned industrial sites, vacant lots,
  or contaminated properties. Often combined with EDA for redevelopment.

SECTION 4: U.S. DEPARTMENT OF HOUSING & URBAN DEVELOPMENT (HUD) PROGRAMS
=============================================================================

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
COMMUNITY DEVELOPMENT BLOCK GRANT (CDBG)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
CFDA: 14.218 (Entitlement), 14.228 (State / Non-Entitlement)
Agency: HUD / State Agency
FY2025 Funding: ~$3.3 billion
Entitlement Cities: Cities ≥ 50,000 population OR principal cities of MSAs receive
  direct annual allocations from HUD (formula: poverty, housing age, population)
Non-Entitlement Communities: Cities < 50,000 apply to state CDBG program
  (each state has own NOFO, priorities, and award process)
Award Range: Entitlement: $400K–$100M+ (formula); Non-Entitlement: $50K–$2M (competitive)
Match: None required (but leverage encouraged)
LMI Requirement: At least 70% of CDBG expenditures must meet a National Objective:
  - Benefit low/moderate income persons (LMI — household income ≤ 80% AMI)
  - Eliminate slum/blight conditions
  - Urgent need (disaster response)
Eligible Activities: Affordable housing rehabilitation, public facilities (community centers,
  senior centers, parks in LMI areas), public services (up to 15% cap), economic development,
  infrastructure in LMI census tracts, code enforcement, clearance/demolition
Key Note: Non-entitlement cities must demonstrate LMI benefit through area benefit
  (census tract ≥ 51% LMI households) or limited clientele (serving LMI individuals).
State Programs: Illinois DCEO CDBG; Texas GLO CDBG; California HCD CDBG

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
HOME INVESTMENT PARTNERSHIPS PROGRAM
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
CFDA: 14.239
Agency: HUD
FY2025 Funding: ~$1.5 billion
Purpose: Expand affordable housing — homeownership, rental, rehabilitation
Eligible: Participating Jurisdictions (PJs) — cities ≥ 65,000 or consortia
Match: 25% required from non-federal sources (waived for distressed)
Eligible Activities: New construction, rehabilitation, down-payment assistance,
  tenant-based rental assistance (TBRA), Community Housing Development Organizations (CHDOs)

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
CHOICE NEIGHBORHOODS INITIATIVE
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
CFDA: 14.889
Agency: HUD
FY2025 Funding: $100M
Award Range: Planning grants $500K; Implementation grants $30–50M
Purpose: Transform distressed public/HUD-assisted housing and surrounding neighborhoods
Eligible: PHAs, local governments, non-profits; must have distressed HUD-assisted housing

SECTION 5: U.S. DEPARTMENT OF AGRICULTURE (USDA) PROGRAMS
=============================================================================

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
RURAL DEVELOPMENT — WATER & ENVIRONMENTAL PROGRAMS
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Water & Waste Water Direct Loans & Grants (CFDA: 10.760):
  Eligibility: Communities with ≤ 10,000 population in rural areas
  Award Range: Loans up to $50M; grants up to $2M (for lowest-income communities)
  Purpose: Water/wastewater system construction, repair, and replacement
  Income Criteria: Grants available when MHI ≤ 80% of state nonmetro median income

Water & Waste Water Guaranteed Loans (CFDA: 10.761):
  Eligibility: Communities with ≤ 10,000 population
  Award Range: Up to $50M guaranteed loan through approved lenders

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
RURAL DEVELOPMENT — COMMUNITY FACILITIES
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Community Facilities Direct Loan & Grant (CFDA: 10.766):
  Eligibility: Rural areas and towns ≤ 20,000 population
  Award Range: Grants up to $500K–$3M depending on income level; loans any amount
  Grant eligibility based on MHI:
    - If MHI ≤ 90% of state nonmetro median → highest grant percentage (up to 75%)
    - If MHI 91–100% of state nonmetro median → reduced grant
    - If MHI > 100% → loans only (no grant)
  Eligible Facilities: Libraries, hospitals, fire stations, community centers,
    schools, childcare, municipal buildings, emergency services
  Application: Through local USDA Rural Development office; no competitive NOFO;
    application accepted year-round (subject to funding availability)

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
RURAL BUSINESS DEVELOPMENT GRANTS (RBDG)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
CFDA: 10.351
Eligibility: Rural areas ≤ 50,000 population
Award Range: $10K–$500K
Purpose: Technical and financial assistance for small rural businesses,
  rural entrepreneurs, economic development planning
Application: Annual NOFO; through USDA State Rural Development office

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
RECONNECT PROGRAM (Broadband)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
CFDA: 10.886
Agency: USDA Rural Development
IIJA Funding: $2.0 billion
Award Range: $100K–$25M per project
Eligibility: Rural areas where 90%+ of households lack 100/20 Mbps service
Purpose: Build broadband infrastructure in unserved/underserved rural areas
Match: 25% minimum (higher match = better scoring)

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
RURAL ENERGY FOR AMERICA PROGRAM (REAP)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
CFDA: 10.868
Eligibility: Agricultural producers + rural small businesses + rural utilities in
  areas ≤ 50,000 population
Award Range: Grants up to 50% of project cost; loans up to 75%
Purpose: Renewable energy systems, energy efficiency improvements

PUBLIC WORKS & ECONOMIC ADJUSTMENT ASSISTANCE
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Agency: EDA / U.S. Department of Commerce
Annual Funding: ~$500M

  Best for communities with manufacturing decline, plant closures, or persistent poverty.

SECTION 8: INFRASTRUCTURE INVESTMENT & JOBS ACT (IIJA/BIL) SUMMARY
=============================================================================

The Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act (PL 117-58), signed November 2021,
provides $1.2 trillion over 5 years, of which $550 billion is NEW investment.
Key municipal-relevant programs:

TRANSPORTATION (Title I-IV):
  - RAISE Discretionary Grants: $7.5B
  - Safe Streets & Roads for All (SS4A): $5B
  - Bridge Investment Program: $12.5B
  - PROTECT: $8.7B
  - Carbon Reduction: $6.4B
  - CMAQ: $11.5B
  - TAP: $9B

WATER & ENVIRONMENT:
  - Lead Service Line Replacement (DWSRF): $15B
  - Water Contamination (CWSRF + PFAS): $11.7B
  - Flood Mitigation Assistance (FMA): $3.5B
  - BRIC: $1B/year

BROADBAND:
  - BEAD (via states): $42.45B
  - ReConnect (USDA): $2B
  - Middle Mile: $1B

ENERGY:
  - Grid Resilience: $10.5B
  - EECBG: $550M
  - EV Charging (NEVI): $5B (formula to states)

For every single one of these IIJA programs, the application portal is:
  - Grants.gov (search by CFDA or program name)
  - SAM.gov (verify active registration first)
  - Federal agency program pages (USDOT/FEMA/EPA/USDA/DOE websites)

SECTION 9: INFLATION REDUCTION ACT (IRA) CLIMATE & ENERGY PROGRAMS
=============================================================================

The Inflation Reduction Act (PL 117-169, August 2022) provides ~$369B for
climate and clean energy investment. Key municipal programs:

GREENHOUSE GAS REDUCTION FUND (GGRF):
  Total: $27B; administered by EPA
  - National Clean Investment Fund: $14B to nonprofit lenders
  - Clean Communities Investment Accelerator: $6B for low-income/tribal
  - Solar for All: $7B for rooftop solar in low-income communities
  Municipalities access through EPA-selected intermediary lenders

ENVIRONMENTAL JUSTICE (IRA Section 60201+):
  - EPA EJ Grants: $3B
  - Thriving Communities Technical Assistance: $400M

CLEAN ENERGY INVESTMENT FOR MUNICIPALITIES:
  IRA provides direct-pay (refundable tax credits) for municipalities:
  - 30% ITC for rooftop solar on municipal buildings
  - 30% for EV charging infrastructure (municipal fleets)
  - Additional 10% for Energy Communities (coal/fossil fuel regions)
  - Additional 10% for Domestic Content (American-made equipment)
  Key: Municipalities can receive direct payment of the credit amount as cash
    under IRA Section 6417 (Direct Pay) — no private partnership required.

CLEAN VEHICLE CREDITS:
  - Commercial Clean Vehicle Credit: Up to $40K per heavy-duty vehicle
    (municipal trucks, transit buses)
  - Municipal transit agencies: EV bus funding through FTA programs

SECTION 12: HOW TO SEARCH FOR GRANTS (ANY CITY)
=============================================================================

PRIMARY RESOURCES:
  1. Grants.gov — All federal grant opportunities (search by agency, CFDA, keyword)
  2. SAM.gov — Registration and contract opportunities
  3. USA Spending (usaspending.gov) — Track what similar cities have received
  4. State grants portal — Every state has a grants catalog (e.g., Illinois GATA, Texas TPASS)
  5. CFDA (now Assistance Listings) — Comprehensive database at sam.gov/content/assistance-listings

USEFUL SEARCH STRATEGIES ON GRANTS.GOV:
  - Search by CFDA number (most precise)
  - Filter by eligible applicant: "Local Government"
  - Filter by category: Transportation, Environment, Community Development
  - Set status: "Posted" (open) + subscription alerts for new NOFOs

STATE-BY-STATE CDBG ADMINISTRATORS:
  Illinois: DCEO (dceo.illinois.gov)
  Texas: GLO (glo.texas.gov)
  California: HCD (hcd.ca.gov)
  Florida: DEO (floridajobs.org)
  New York: HCR (hcr.ny.gov)
  Ohio: ODSA (development.ohio.gov)
  Pennsylvania: DCED (dced.pa.gov)
  Michigan: MEDC (michiganbusiness.org)
