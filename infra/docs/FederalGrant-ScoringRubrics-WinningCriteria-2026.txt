SECTION 1: USDOT RAISE FY2026 — SCORING CRITERIA & POINT MAXIMIZATION
=============================================================================

RAISE uses a merit-based review by a panel of USDOT career staff and subject matter
experts. Applications are scored on 7 primary criteria, each with sub-criteria.
Understanding exactly what reviewers score = the difference between award and rejection.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
RAISE EVALUATION CRITERIA (equal weighting across all 7 — no published point scale;
reviewers rate each criterion as "highly met", "met", "partially met", "not met")
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

CRITERION 1 — SAFETY (Weight: HIGHEST — Secretary has stated as top priority)
What reviewers score:
  a) Does the project address a documented safety problem?
     → WINNING response: Cite crash data with specific numbers (fatal, injury, PDO crashes over 5 years)
     → Reference FHWA's High-Risk Rural Roads designation or state High-Injury Network listing
     → Provide crash rate vs. state/national average (if your rate is 2× average, say so explicitly)
  b) Does the project use proven safety countermeasures?
     → Reference FHWA's Proven Safety Countermeasures guide (RIRO, HAWK, median barriers, etc.)
     → If adaptive signals, cite NCHRP research showing % crash reduction
  c) Does the project serve vulnerable road users?
     → Pedestrians, cyclists, school children, elderly, transit users — DOCUMENT specifically
     → "X schoolchildren cross this intersection daily" > "the area has pedestrian activity"
WINNING LANGUAGE: "This project directly addresses Buffalo Grove's highest-crash intersection
  with 87 documented crashes (FY2019-FY2023), including 2 fatal and 31 injury crashes,
  at a crash rate of 1.42 per million VMT vs. the Illinois statewide average of 0.68 —
  placing it on IDOT's High-Injury Network and triggering the IDOT 2022 IL-83 Safety Corridor Study."

CRITERION 2 — ENVIRONMENTAL SUSTAINABILITY (Weight: HIGH)
What reviewers score:
  a) GHG reduction — quantify CO2 tons reduced per year; use FHWA emissions calculator
     → Signal retiming can reduce idling emissions; quantify specifically
     → Active transportation (bike/ped) replaces vehicle trips; estimate VMT reduction
  b) Climate resilience — does the project withstand future climate scenarios?
     → Cite NOAA Atlas 14 precipitation data; design to 50-yr or 100-yr storm event
     → Reference local climate vulnerability assessment if available
  c) Environmental justice benefits (overlap with equity criterion)
     → Note: Projects in Justice40 communities get extra consideration here
WINNING LANGUAGE: "The Aptakisic/IL-83 signal optimization and protected bike lane to
  Metra Prairie View is projected to eliminate approximately 847 annual vehicle trips
  (based on 2024 station boardings and mode shift benchmarks from comparable Metra-connected
  bike infrastructure), reducing annual CO2 emissions by an estimated 142 metric tons."

CRITERION 3 — QUALITY OF LIFE (Weight: MEDIUM-HIGH)
What reviewers score:
  a) Access to jobs, education, healthcare, and essential services
  b) Multimodal connectivity — does the project connect to transit, trails, other networks?
  c) Livability improvements — community character, placemaking, noise/air quality
WINNING LANGUAGE: Specifically name what destinations are connected:
  "The Prairie View Metra Station (1,240 daily boardings, North Central Service) is currently
  inaccessible to 12,000 residents on the east side of IL-83 without a vehicle. This project
  creates the first protected pedestrian/cyclist crossing, closing a critical last-mile gap
  and enabling transit access for residents within a 2.2-mile radius."

CRITERION 4 — EQUITY (Weight: HIGH — FY2026 elevated to major factor)
What reviewers score:
  a) Does the project serve disadvantaged communities? (Justice40, LMI, minority, elderly, disabled)
  b) Is there a community engagement process that included disadvantaged groups?
  c) Does the applicant have an Equity Action Plan?
WINNING STRATEGY:
  → Use USDOT's EJScreen and Census data to identify LMI census tracts within project area
  → Document % of residents within 1/2 mile who are LMI, elderly, or persons of color
  → Reference Section 508/ADA compliance as baseline equity — then go further
  → If community has senior population > 15%, mention ADA sidewalk improvements explicitly
  → Cite any formal public engagement: meetings, surveys, bilingual outreach
CRITICAL: Don't just say "the project serves equity." Name specific populations with data.
  "17.4% of Buffalo Grove residents are age 65+; this intersection's LOS F and missing
  pedestrian crossings create a documented barrier for 7,200 seniors accessing transit,
  medical facilities, and the commercial district."

CRITERION 5 — ECONOMIC STRENGTH (Weight: MEDIUM)
What reviewers score:
  a) Does the project support economic vitality (jobs, freight, business access)?
  b) Is there a credible benefit-cost analysis (BCA) showing positive net benefit?
  c) Does the project leverage private investment or economic development?
WINNING STRATEGY:
  → Always submit a BCA (even if not required for < $5M rural awards)
  → BCR ≥ 2.0 is considered "strong"; BCR ≥ 3.0 is "excellent"
  → Quantify business ADT served; connect to freight flows if applicable
  → Name specific businesses or employers served: "the project serves the primary
     commercial district with 210 businesses and 4,200 employees"
  → Use USDOT BCA Toolkit v6 (free download) with Illinois-specific values

CRITERION 6 — STATE OF GOOD REPAIR (Weight: MEDIUM)
What reviewers score:
  a) Is the existing infrastructure in documented poor condition?
  b) Does the project bring assets to a state of good repair?
WINNING LANGUAGE: Use objective condition metrics:
  → Pavement Condition Index (PCI): PCI < 40 = "Very Poor"; cite actual PCI score
  → Bridge condition: "Structurally deficient" or "Functionally obsolete" designation
  → Signal age: "Traffic signal installed 1992; no longer maintainable"
  → Crash trend: "Crash frequency increased 34% over the past 5 years"

CRITERION 7 — INNOVATION (Weight: LOWER — differentiator, not disqualifier)
What reviewers score:
  a) Does the project use innovative technologies or delivery methods?
  b) Does it serve as a model for other communities?
WINNING ADDITIONS:
  → Adaptive signal control technology (ASCT) — cite % crash reduction and emissions benefit
  → Connected vehicle / V2I infrastructure readiness
  → Design-build or P3 delivery if it reduces cost or schedule
  → Document how the project will be a replicable model: "Buffalo Grove's documented
     methodology will be shared via CMAP's Transportation Alternatives clearinghouse"

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
RAISE APPLICATION COMMON FATAL FLAWS (from FOIA-released debrief letters)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
1. NO BCA or BCA with BCR < 1.0 — reviewers cannot recommend funding without positive net benefit
2. Project not NEPA-ready — if right-of-way not secured and no environmental clearance, score drops significantly
3. Match is "pending" not "committed" — letters of commitment from matching partners required
4. Benefits are stated, not quantified — "will improve safety" vs. "will reduce crashes by 28% based on HSIP countermeasure effectiveness rates"
5. Vague equity section — listing demographics without explaining how the project specifically benefits them
6. Overlong narrative — USDOT RAISE page limits are strict; reviewers penalize for padding
7. Missing MAP-21/BIL compliance certifications — include all required assurances even if boilerplate

SECTION 2: FEMA BRIC FY2026 — SCORING MATRIX & MAXIMIZATION
=============================================================================

BRIC uses a 200-point scoring matrix evaluated by FEMA Regional staff + HQ review.
The scoring categories and approximate point allocations (from FY2024 NOFO):

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
BRIC SCORING MATRIX (FY2024/2025 NOFO — FY2026 expected to be similar)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

CATEGORY A: COST-EFFECTIVENESS (up to 30 points)
  A1. Benefit-Cost Ratio (BCR): 
      BCR ≥ 1.0: minimum (required for consideration)
      BCR 2.0–3.0: 15 points
      BCR ≥ 3.0: 20 points
      BCR ≥ 5.0: 25 points (rare but documented for flood warning systems with high property value areas)
  A2. Cost reasonableness: Independent cost estimate vs. project estimate within 20% = 10 points
  How to maximize:
    → Use Hazus-MH to model avoided building damage (free FEMA software)
    → Include avoided emergency response costs, reduced flood insurance premiums, reduced traffic disruption
    → For warning systems: calculate "avoided evacuation failure" costs using FEMA methodology
    → Tip: Flood warning systems in high-value suburban areas (like BG) often achieve BCR 4-8+

CATEGORY B: FEASIBILITY & EFFECTIVENESS (up to 50 points)
  B1. Technical feasibility — engineering analysis required (30 points):
      → Must show system/project will work as designed
      → For green infrastructure: include hydrologic modeling (HEC-HMS) showing runoff reduction
      → For warning systems: include sensor coverage map and notification radius analysis
      → For lift stations: include hydraulic analysis showing performance at design storm
  B2. Organizational capacity (20 points):
      → Cite prior federal grant compliance record (Buffalo Grove = $5.5M SIIP compliance)
      → Staff credentials: name specific staff managing grant (e.g., Village Engineer credentials)
      → Prior FEMA award compliance record — any prior HMGP/BRIC awards with clean closeouts
  HOW TO MAXIMIZE B2: List SPECIFIC past grants with award amounts, closeout dates, and
    "zero audit findings" language. Buffalo Grove: "The Village has received and successfully
    closed out [X] federal grants totaling $[Y] with zero audit findings or compliance issues."

CATEGORY C: COMMUNITY RESILIENCE (up to 40 points)
  C1. NFIP participation and CRS class (up to 15 points):
      → CRS Class 10: 0 points; CRS Class 9: 3 pts; Class 8: 6 pts; Class 7: 9 pts; Class 6+: 15 pts
      → Buffalo Grove CRS Class 7 = 9 points automatically
  C2. LHMP integration — project must be in current LHMP (10 points):
      → Must cite LHMP page/section where this project appears
      → "Buffalo Grove's 2022 LHMP, Ordinance #2022-16, Priority Action Item #3: Buffalo Creek
         Flood Early Warning System" — this exact language = 10/10 points
  C3. Community adoption and community buy-in (15 points):
      → Village Board resolution supporting the project
      → Letters from Lake County Emergency Management, Lake County IDNR
      → Community engagement documentation (public meetings, survey results)

CATEGORY D: LEVERAGED FUNDING (up to 40 points)
  D1. Non-federal match above the 25% minimum:
      Match = 25%: 0 bonus points (minimum)
      Match = 30%: 10 points
      Match = 40%: 20 points
      Match = 50%+: 30 points
      Match = 75%+: 40 points (transformational)
  D2. Prior federal investment in the area (10 points):
      → Prior federal investment in the watershed or corridor = shows established federal relationship
      → Buffalo Grove: "$5.5M SIIP + $[HMGP prior awards] = demonstrated federal investment in watershed"
  HOW TO MAXIMIZE: Increase your non-federal match percentage. Buffalo Grove's $15.4M capital
    reserves make it feasible to offer 40-50% match — this alone can add 20-30 points.

CATEGORY E: EQUITY & ENVIRONMENTAL JUSTICE (up to 40 points) — NEW IN FY2024+
  E1. Project serves Justice40/CEJST disadvantaged community (20 points):
      → Full 20 points if project is IN or directly benefits a CEJST-designated area
      → Check: screeningtool.geoplatform.gov — identify if any affected census tracts qualify
      → Even if the project area isn't 100% Justice40, document adjacent disadvantaged areas served
  E2. Community engagement with underserved populations (10 points):
      → Bilingual community meetings, ADA-accessible public input process
      → Document specific engagement events with attendance figures
  E3. Equity benefits — quantify who benefits (10 points):
      → "X low-income households, Y elderly residents, Z residents with disabilities benefit"
  TIP: Buffalo Grove's 8.2% disability population (3,403 residents) and 17.4% elderly are
    strong equity metrics even though the city is middle-income overall.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
BRIC FATAL FLAWS (confirmed from FEMA BRIC debrief feedback)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
1. LHMP not current or project not listed in LHMP — automatic disqualification
2. BCR < 1.0 — not recommended for funding
3. State BSIR not submitted on time (state has separate deadline before FEMA national deadline)
4. Duplicate projects — BRIC will not fund a project already funded by HMGP in same mitigation area
5. "General planning" or "studies" — BRIC funds construction projects; pure planning has its own category
6. Missing cost effectiveness analysis (not just BCR — must show methodology in full)

SECTION 3: SS4A (SAFE STREETS & ROADS FOR ALL) SCORING CRITERIA FY2026
=============================================================================

SS4A uses a merit-based competitive process with published evaluation criteria:

ACTION PLAN GRANTS (Phase 1 — for cities without a safety action plan):
Primary evaluation factors:
  1. Strength of commitment to safety: Does leadership (elected officials) have a public
     commitment to Vision Zero / Toward Zero Deaths?
     → Resolution from city council committing to zero traffic deaths = strong signal
  2. Engagement approach: How will underserved/vulnerable communities be included?
     → Named specific populations, languages, accessibility accommodations
  3. Use of proven methodology: Does the plan follow USDOT's Safe System Approach?
     → Safe System = Safe Roads + Safe Speeds + Safe Vehicles + Safe People + Safe Post-Crash Care
  4. Baseline data: Does the applicant have crash data demonstrating the need?
     → 5-year crash history, pedestrian/cyclist crash data, KABCO severity

IMPLEMENTATION GRANTS (Phase 2 — funded action plan already exists):
Primary evaluation factors (USDOT SS4A FY2024 NOFO criteria):
  1. Evidence of local commitment: Existing safety action plan (required), budget allocation,
     Vision Zero goal, city council resolutions
  2. Project location analysis: Are project locations drawn from the action plan's high-injury
     network or priority corridors?
  3. Equity integration: Do project locations align with areas where vulnerable populations
     are disproportionately involved in crashes?
  4. Proven countermeasures: Are selected improvements from FHWA's Proven Safety
     Countermeasures list or NCHRP-tested solutions?
  5. Partnerships: Multi-agency coordination (police, schools, transit, health dept)?

SS4A WINNING DIFFERENTIATORS:
  → Two-city partnerships (apply together with neighboring municipality) = stronger
  → School Safe Routes to School integration = equity and safety in one
  → Transit agency co-applicant = multimodal equity
  → Vision Zero coalition with local health dept = cross-sector innovation

SECTION 4: HUD CDBG — STATE PROGRAM SCORING CRITERIA
=============================================================================

Each state CDBG program has its own scoring — here are the most common factors:

ILLINOIS DCEO STATE CDBG SCORING (FY2025 Infrastructure Category):
  Community Need (40 points):
    → % residents below poverty line (ACS data); % LMI households in project area
    → Severity of infrastructure deficiency (age of system, failure rate, health impacts)
    → Documentation of health/safety impacts: boil orders, basement flooding incidents
  Project Impact (30 points):
    → # LMI persons directly benefited (higher = better)
    → % of project area that is LMI (at least 51% required; higher scores better)
    → Removal of slum/blight conditions if applicable
  Capacity & Readiness (20 points):
    → Prior CDBG compliance record (clean = full points)
    → Project design/engineering completeness (30% design = more points)
    → Procurement method identified
  Leverage (10 points):
    → Additional non-CDBG funding committed (local, state, federal other than CDBG)

TEXAS GLO CDBG SCORING (FY2025):
  National Objective Documentation (pass/fail): Must demonstrate LMI benefit
  Need (35 points): Severity of need, population affected
  Benefit (35 points): # LMI persons benefited, income documentation
  Capacity (20 points): Financial health, past performance
  Sustainability (10 points): Long-term maintenance plan

SECTION 5: EPA CWSRF — PRINCIPAL FORGIVENESS SCORING (ILLINOIS)
=============================================================================

IEPA (Illinois EPA) CWSRF Priority System (used to determine grant portion):

Priority Points (0-100) determine position on Fundable List:
  Health-based criteria (50 pts max):
    → Impaired waterway / 303(d) list: 20 pts
    → Drinking water MCL violation: 15 pts
    → Combined sewer overflow (CSO): 15 pts
  Readiness (30 pts max):
    → Plans and specifications complete: 20 pts
    → Site access/land acquisition: 10 pts
  Green Infrastructure Bonus (20 pts):
    → ≥50% green infrastructure components: 20 pts
    → 25-49% green infrastructure: 10 pts

Principal Forgiveness (Grant Component) Thresholds:
  → City population ≤ 1,000: 100% forgiveness
  → City population 1,001–10,000: 55% forgiveness
  → City population 10,001–25,000: 30% forgiveness
  → City population 25,001–50,000: 15% forgiveness
  → City population 50,001–100,000: 5% forgiveness
  → City population > 100,000: No forgiveness (standard loan rates only)
  → Additional forgiveness if MHI ≤ 80% of Illinois median: add 25%
  Note: Buffalo Grove at 41,496 = 15% forgiveness on face value; however, the
    green infrastructure bonus (20 pts) may push project to top of list faster.

SECTION 6: EDA PUBLIC WORKS — SCORING & DISTRESS CRITERIA
=============================================================================

EDA ECONOMIC DISTRESS DESIGNATION (required for Public Works grant access):
A community qualifies if ONE of these is true:
  a) Unemployment rate ≥ 1.5× national average for 24 months (national avg ~4.0% → threshold ≥ 6.0%)
  b) Per capita income ≤ 80% of national average (national avg ~$61K → threshold ≤ $48.8K)
  c) Special needs designation (recent major plant closure, natural disaster, defense base closure, chronic poverty)

EDA SCORING FACTORS (Public Works category):
  Economic Distress Level (40%): How distressed is the area? Quantified using EDA index
  Project Impact (30%): Jobs created/retained, private investment leveraged, wage levels
  Feasibility (20%): Engineering readiness, permit status, financial capacity for O&M
  Consistency with CEDS (10%): Aligns with region's Comprehensive Economic Development Strategy?

IMPORTANT: EDA requires a current CEDS from the Economic Development District (EDD).
  Check if your metro/county has an active EDD with a CEDS — this is the first step.
  Chicago region EDD: Chicago Metropolitan Agency for Planning (CMAP) maintains the CEDS.

SECTION 7: UNIVERSAL GRANT-WINNING PRINCIPLES (CROSS-PROGRAM)
=============================================================================

These principles consistently separate winning from losing applications across ALL programs:

PRINCIPLE 1 — QUANTIFY EVERYTHING
  "The project will improve safety" → LOSES
  "The project will reduce crashes by 28% (based on FHWA Proven Safety Countermeasures data
  for protected bike lanes at similar intersections) — reducing annual crash costs by $1.4M
  (using FHWA crash cost methodology: $1.68M/fatality, $125K/injury, $20K/PDO)"  → WINS

PRINCIPLE 2 — DEMONSTRATE READINESS
  Winning projects can break ground within 18 months. Signals:
  → Design complete or at 30%+
  → NEPA: categorical exclusion documented or EA underway
  → Right-of-way: acquired or in condemnation; no barriers
  → Permits: applied for or obtained (Army Corps 404, state permits)
  → Local match: committed in writing (budget resolution + letter)

PRINCIPLE 3 — SHOW YOUR TRACK RECORD
  One sentence that wins points: "The City of [X] has received and successfully administered
  $[total] in federal grants over [Y] years with a zero-finding audit record, as documented
  in the attached Single Audit reports."
  This closes the "can they execute?" question reviewers always ask.

PRINCIPLE 4 — ESTABLISH URGENCY + CONSEQUENCE
  Reviewers must justify every award to leadership. Give them the narrative:
  → "If not funded this cycle, the project will be delayed 18–24 months due to [specific reason]"
  → "Each year of delay costs $X in emergency repairs / $Y in crash costs / Z injuries"
  → "The existing [infrastructure] is [age] years old and [X events] have already occurred"

PRINCIPLE 5 — USE THE PROGRAM'S OWN LANGUAGE
  Copy exact phrases from the NOFO into your narrative. If the NOFO says "climate resilience"
  18 times, use "climate resilience" in your application 18 times. Reviewers are checking
  criteria boxes — make it obvious which box each paragraph is checking.

PRINCIPLE 6 — PARTNERS MULTIPLY COMPETITIVENESS
  Multi-entity applications consistently score higher. For every grant:
  → Letter of support from state DOT or state emergency management
  → Letter from congressional delegation (staff contact your district office 2-3 weeks out)
  → Regional planning agency (MPO, COG) endorsement
  → Adjacent municipality (if project benefits both cities)
  → Private sector partner (freight company, developer, hospital) if project serves them

PRINCIPLE 7 — AVOID THE FIVE REVIEWER RED FLAGS
  1. "Conceptual" project — no design, no site control, no permits initiated
  2. Unfunded match — "we plan to fund the match through future bonds" (not committed)
  3. Single-use justification — only one reason to fund (strong apps have 3+ independent justifications)
  4. Vague benefits — no quantification, no data sources cited
  5. Grammatical errors / unclear technical writing — signals organizational capacity issues

SECTION 8: HOW TO CALCULATE A BENEFIT-COST ANALYSIS (BCA) FOR MUNICIPAL GRANTS
=============================================================================

BCA is required for RAISE ($5M+ awards) and strongly preferred for BRIC.
Many cities skip it or do it poorly — a strong BCA is a major competitive differentiator.

STANDARD BENEFIT CATEGORIES FOR TRANSPORTATION PROJECTS (RAISE/HSIP):
  Safety Benefits (typically largest component):
    → Value of Statistical Life (VSL): USDOT current value = $13.2M per fatality (2024)
    → Comprehensive injury cost: $1.68M/fatality, $1.07M/serious injury, $125K/moderate injury,
      $38K/minor injury, $20K/PDO (FHWA 2024 unit values)
    → Calculate: Annual crashes × crash type distribution × countermeasure effectiveness rate × VSL
    → Countermeasure effectiveness rates: FHWA Proven Safety Countermeasures guide
      - Protected intersection: −30 to −50% pedestrian crash reduction
      - Adaptive signals: −15 to −25% total crash reduction
      - Roundabout (major conversion): −35 to −75% injury crash reduction

  Travel Time Benefits:
    → USDOT Value of Travel Time: $17.80/hour (local travel, 2024)
    → Calculate: ADT × % vehicles delayed × average delay reduction × days/year × $17.80/hr
    → For congested intersections: delay reduction of 30-60 seconds/vehicle is typical

  Emissions Benefits:
    → Social cost of carbon: $51/metric ton CO2 (USDOT 2024)
    → Reduced idling from signal optimization: estimate from FHWA emissions calculator

  Pavement/Infrastructure Life Extension:
    → Preventive maintenance extends pavement life; avoid $X/lane-mile reconstruction cost
    → NPV calculation: deferred reconstruction cost × probability of deferral

STANDARD BENEFIT CATEGORIES FOR FLOOD/STORMWATER PROJECTS (BRIC/HMGP):
  Property Damage Reduction (typically largest component):
    → Use Hazus-MH software (free FEMA tool) to estimate annualized loss reduction
    → Alternatively: historical flood damage data × effectiveness reduction factor
    → "Annualized loss" = expected damage per year averaged over storm probability distribution
  Emergency Response Cost Reduction:
    → Cost per flood event: emergency response, road closures, utility restoration
    → Multiply by annual event probability
  Flood Insurance Premium Reduction:
    → CRS class improvement (Class 7 → Class 6): additional 5% premium reduction
    → Multiply by number of insured structures × average premium × premium reduction %
  Economic disruption avoided:
    → Lost business revenue during flooding
    → Traffic diversion costs
  Property value increase:
    → Floodplain removal from flood zone: average 5-15% property value increase
    → Not typically included in federal BCA but can be noted in economic impact narrative

BCA DISCOUNT RATE AND ANALYSIS PERIOD:
  → USDOT/FEMA standard: 7% real discount rate (or 3% as sensitivity check)
  → Analysis period: 20 years (transportation); 30-50 years (infrastructure/flood control)
  → NPV formula: sum(Benefits_t / (1.07)^t) - sum(Costs_t / (1.07)^t) for t=1 to N years
  → BCR = NPV(Benefits) / NPV(Costs); BCR ≥ 1.0 = positive net benefit
  → WINNING BCR: ≥ 2.5 for transportation; ≥ 3.0 for flood mitigation (achievable with good data)

FREE BCA TOOLS:
  → USDOT BCA Toolkit v6: transportation.gov/office-policy/transportation-policy/benefit-cost-analysis-guidance
  → Hazus-MH: fema.gov/hazus (flood, earthquake, hurricane — free FEMA desktop software)
  → FHWA Crash Cost tables: safety.fhwa.dot.gov/hsip/spm/conversion_tbl/
  → FHWA Emissions Calculators: fhwa.dot.gov/environment/air_quality/

SECTION 9: GRANT APPLICATION REVIEW PROCESS — WHAT HAPPENS AFTER YOU SUBMIT
=============================================================================

Understanding the review timeline helps with follow-up strategy:

RAISE REVIEW TIMELINE (typical):
  Week 1-4: Completeness check (USDOT staff verifies all required components present)
  Month 1-2: Merit review by USDOT mode experts (FHWA, FTA, FRA, MARAD)
  Month 2-4: Interagency and political review (USDOT Secretary's office, OMB, White House)
  Month 4-6: Award announcements (Secretary signature letters to awardees)
  Month 6-12: Grant agreement negotiation
  Total timeline: 6-12 months from application deadline to execution

BRIC REVIEW TIMELINE:
  January: Application deadline (subapplication to state HSEMD)
  January-March: State review and ranking
  March-April: State submits selected applications to FEMA Region
  April-July: FEMA Regional review
  July-October: FEMA HQ review and award recommendation
  October-December: Award announcements
  Total: 10-12 months from deadline

WHAT TO DO WHILE UNDER REVIEW:
  → Congressional liaison: Have your US Representative / Senator's office inquire on your behalf
    at the 6-month mark — this is standard practice and doesn't hurt your application
  → Community announcements: Village Board resolution "expressing intent to accept award if granted"
    signals local commitment and gets into the public record
  → Design advancement: Advance design from 30% to 60% during review — demonstrates readiness
    and positions you to execute quickly upon award
  → Do NOT contact the program office asking for status — this can backfire

IF YOUR APPLICATION IS DENIED:
  → Always request a debrief call with the program office (this is your right for RAISE/BRIC)
  → FOIA the reviewer score sheets (takes 3-6 months but reveals exactly where you lost points)
  → Incorporate feedback into your resubmission for the NEXT cycle
  → RAISE resubmission success rate is approximately 40-60% on the second attempt
